L-meta-1031: independent mathematical review of the three-child reduction

Status: PAPER_REVIEWED. This note is an independent semantic review, not a
Lean-check record and not a proof of the open circuit-normalization child.
The candidate reviewed is reduction-candidate-prepare.lean.

1. Scope of the three children

The first child asks for an existential minimum monotone circuit computing
the graph edge function, with every AND gate owning at least one graph edge
and each such owned-edge family pairwise intersecting. Minimum means total
gate count among all fan-in-at-most-two monotone circuits computing the same
function. It does not assert a property of every minimum circuit.

The second child identifies the finite Owns definition from PEqualNP.Defs
with the exact strict-ancestor predicate in the posted statement. This is a
reusable semantic interface, independent of the difficult normal form.

The third child is the graph-theoretic fact that a nonempty pairwise-
intersecting family of edges in a triangle-free simple graph has a common
endpoint. Its family predicate need not be symmetric.

The assembly uses one circuit throughout. IsMinimalMonoCircuit expands to
the posted total-size minimality and Monotone2 hypotheses. graphFun G empty
is exactly the posted edge function. The valid-gate and AND hypotheses of
the second child supply the extra conjuncts included in Owns. The third
child is applied to Adj(u,v) and Owns(n,C,t,u,v), retaining nonemptiness.

2. Finite cone computation agrees with strict reachability

In fact, for arbitrary n,C,i,j, without monotonicity, fan-in, or circuit
well-formedness assumptions, InCone n C i j is equivalent to TransGen of
the relation in the posted statement, from wire n+i to wire n+j.

Proof invariant after processing m gates: the list of cones has length m;
entry j<m is the strict ancestor set of gate j; earlier entries do not
change when the next gate is appended. At gate j, coneStep keeps exactly
the input wires w satisfying n<=w<n+j. It adds the predecessor k=w-n and
all members of the already-computed cone of k. Thus membership is exactly

  exists k<j, (n+k is an input of gate j) and
               (i=k or i belongs to the cone of k).

This is the last-edge decomposition of strict reachability. Every step of
the posted relation increases the wire index, so any intermediate gate is
earlier than its successor. Raw inputs cannot appear between a gate-index
source and the target, and invalid/future reads are excluded on both sides.
When j is out of range, the finite cone is empty and the reachability
relation has no final edge into n+j. These observations also handle i>=j.

3. Triangle-free edge-family argument

Choose an edge uv in the nonempty family. If every family edge contains u,
use u as center. Otherwise choose a family edge missing u. Pairwise
intersection forces it to contain v, so write its other endpoint as w.
Simplicity gives w!=v, and the choice gives w!=u.

Suppose a family edge misses v. To intersect uv, it must contain u. To
intersect vw, it must contain w. Hence it is the edge uw, which together
with uv and vw forms a triangle. This is impossible. Every family edge
therefore contains v, which is the required common endpoint.

Neither finiteness nor C4-freeness is used by this graph step. Nonemptiness
is required to produce a center when the vertex type is empty. The
nonempty circuit condition also excludes AND gates with no owned edges;
the existing HasStarProfile definition alone permits those gates.

4. Why a separate productivity lemma is not treated as routine

A monotone AND gate with no owned graph edge can still filter false
positives introduced by an earlier AND gate. For example, on four inputs
a,b,c,d the five-gate circuit

  ((a OR b) AND (c OR d)) AND (a OR c)

computes ac OR ad OR bc, the edge function of a four-vertex path. The
first AND owns all three graph edges and also accepts the nonedge bd.
The last AND owns no graph edge because the first AND is a true ancestor
on every positive edge input. Deleting that last filter gives a false
positive at bd. This particular circuit is not minimum (a four-gate
circuit exists), so it is not a counterexample to the proposed child.
It does refute a naive rule that every non-owning AND can simply be
bypassed. Shared dirty ancestors and several downstream filters are the
fanout obstruction that a genuine productivity normalization must handle.

Splitting productivity from pairwise-intersection could be useful if an
explicit size-preserving local exchange is known. A general assertion
that every defective minimum productive circuit has some globally better
normal form would mostly restate the remaining existence problem, while
adding constraints such as preserving every other gate's ownership could
be unjustifiably strong. No such local exchange is claimed here.

The first child retains both triangle-free and C4-free hypotheses. A
C4-free-only strengthening may be interesting, but this review supplies
no proof or independent evidence for it.

5. Mathematical progress represented by the reduction

The reduction isolates an implementation bridge and an elementary graph
lemma, both independently useful and provably true. The existence of a
minimum circuit with productive pairwise-intersecting ownership remains
the principal open mathematical obligation. Passing the reduction's Lean
check verifies only the assembly from the three children, not that open
normal-form statement.
