L-meta-909: Petersen partial-function geometry and a necessary dirty gate

Status: PAPER_PROOF with exact finite geometry audit. No circuit existence
or nonexistence result is claimed. geometry_audit.py checks all 1024 vertex
subsets and writes geometry-audit.json. It does not run a SAT solver.

1. Exact Petersen coordinates

The posted vertices 0,...,9 correspond to the following two-element
subsets of {0,1,2,3,4}:

  01, 23, 04, 13, 24, 34, 14, 12, 02, 03.

Posted adjacency is exactly disjointness of these pairs. Thus a Petersen
independent set is a pairwise-intersecting family of K5 edges. The maximal
families are the five complete four-edge stars and the ten triangles.
There are 76 independent vertex sets in total, with size distribution
1,10,30,30,5. The maximum independent-set size is four, so the minimum
vertex-cover size is six.

The fifteen zero rows are these maximal independent sets. Their distinct
one-point extensions consist of seventy four-sets and thirty five-sets;
there are no duplicate extensions. Of the seventy four-sets, sixty induce
one Petersen edge and two isolated vertices, and ten induce a K1,3.

2. Every edge has a unique-edge extension

Write an adjacent Petersen pair as ab and cd, with a,b,c,d distinct K5
vertices. The K5 triangle {ac,ad,cd} is a maximal Petersen independent
set. Adjoining ab produces a required positive four-set whose only
Petersen edge is {ab,cd}. The triangle {bc,bd,cd} gives a second witness
with ab as the added vertex, and reversing the roles gives two more.

The finite audit independently finds exactly these four extension
witnesses for each of the fifteen edges, two for each orientation.
All masks and added-vertex records are retained in geometry-audit.json.

3. Thirty positive rows are redundant by monotonicity

A five-point positive row is a K5 star at a, together with an additional
edge bc. It contains the triangle {ab,ac,bc} and a fourth edge ad, where
d is distinct from a,b,c. That four-set is already a required positive
extension of a triangle MIS. Every AndCountCircuit is monotone, so the
seventy four-point positives imply all thirty five-point positives.

The exact point constraints can therefore be reduced to fifteen zeros
and seventy positives. This is a mathematical reduction of row tests;
the first unrestricted model was intentionally left on all 115 rows.

4. All-clean circuits violate the bare-variable budget

Claim. A well-formed AndCountCircuit satisfying the posted partial
function and the at-most-two-gates bare-variable condition has some gate
true on a maximal independent set. The claim does not require size<=6.

Proof. Suppose every gate is false on every maximal independent set.
For gate i write L_i,R_i for its two bare-variable sets and put
B_i=L_i union R_i. Each vertex belongs to some maximal independent set.
Consequently no vertex can belong to both L_i and R_i, since that vertex
alone would make both sides of gate i true on such a set. More generally,
every pair a in L_i,b in R_i must be adjacent: any independent pair can
be extended to a maximal independent set and would make gate i true.

Thus the bare cross-product E_i=L_i times R_i is a Petersen biclique.
If both sides are nonempty, C4-freeness implies that one side is a
singleton. Hence E_i is a nonempty star and |B_i|=|E_i|+1. The audit
enumerates all 110 ordered nonempty bicliques and checks this identity.

There are no output variables: each vertex belongs to a zero MIS. On a
required positive extension U, choose the earliest gate true on U.
Well-formedness makes every gate referenced by its sides earlier, and
therefore false on U. Both sides must instead be activated by bare
variables. The selected gate consequently contributes an E_i edge inside
U. Apply this to a unique-edge extension from Section2: that E_i must
contain the specified Petersen edge. It follows that the bare stars of
the productive gates cover all fifteen Petersen edges.

Let J be the gates with two nonempty bare sets. Choosing one center from
each bare star gives a vertex cover, so |J|>=6. Therefore

  sum_i |B_i| >= sum_{i in J} (|E_i|+1)
               >= 15+|J| >=21.

The exact posted count counts a vertex once per gate when it occurs on
either side. Double counting those incidences gives

  sum_i |B_i| = sum_v #{i : v in L_i or v in R_i} <=10*2=20,

a contradiction. This argument permits empty sides, repeated use of a
previous gate, overlapping bare sets before deriving their impossibility
under the all-clean assumption, arbitrary output-gate subsets, and gates
that do not feed the output. It counts the union of the two bare sets,
exactly as the statement requires.

5. A canonical dirty triangle is a complete symmetry restriction

The previous argument forces a dirty gate. Choose an MIS on which some
gate is true, and take the earliest such gate. Earlier gates are false,
so its left and right sides contain bare witnesses from that MIS. These
two witnesses are equal or form a nonadjacent Petersen pair.

In the K5 coordinates, every such pair extends both to a triangle and to
a four-edge star. For distinct intersecting edges, use their three-point
triangle and their common-endpoint star; a repeated edge can be extended
either way. The same gate is therefore true on a triangle MIS and on a
star MIS, since its bare witnesses suffice without any gate inputs.

Permuting the five K5 vertices induces Petersen automorphisms. These act
transitively on the triangle MIS, so every witness circuit can be
relabeled, without changing any gate count or bare-variable incidence,
to have a dirty gate on the fixed MIS {0,2,6}. A fixed star alternative is
{0,2,8,9}. One need not split existence into the two MIS-size orbits: the
triangle orbit alone is always sufficient for this dirty-gate condition.

This does not justify requiring the first circuit gate to be dirty, a
particular gate index to be dirty, or two fixed MIS to be dirty
simultaneously. It also does not justify eliminating an AND with equal
or overlapping bare sides. For example, an AND copying a bare variable
can distribute it to later gates while using only one bare incidence;
expanding that alias may violate the at-most-two-gates condition.

6. Remaining construction problem

A six-AND solution, if it exists, must use a dirty internal gate and
subsequent filtering. Any output gate is false on every zero row, so a
dirty gate cannot itself be selected by the output. At least one later
gate is needed on a path by which a live dirty gate influences an output.
The counting proof above supplies a necessary structural cut, not a
general lower bound on all six-AND circuits and not a construction.
