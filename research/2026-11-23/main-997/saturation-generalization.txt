SATURATION GENERALIZATION OF THE PG(2,4) PAPER ARGUMENT

Status: paper mathematics, not a Lean theorem or a SAT certificate. The
underlying true-at-Y minterm budget is kernel-checked separately in
formal/MintermSlack.lean. The light-window private count is kernel-checked
in formal/LightCount.lean, the OR-only private count in OrPrivateCount.lean,
its C0 bridge in HeavyReplacement.lean, and the original NOT-window light
bound in NotWindow.lean. RefundCount.lean checks the posted refund and excess
criterion; SmallWindow.lean checks the complete original theorem for |Y|<=2
or for families whose target sets all have size at most4. The full saturation
gate partition and residual-prefix classification remain paper arguments.
Criterion C was independently reviewed by the bounds worker, who found no
flaw; the resulting theorem still has no complete Lean formalization.

All circuit and refund conventions are exactly those of L-meta-997. The
target sets meet pairwise in at most one point. No assumption is made that
two distinct light targets actually intersect.


1. NOTATION FOR A THREE-POINT WINDOW

Assume |Y|=3. A target is heavy if it is disjoint from Y, and light otherwise.
Write d_i=|S_i| and define:

  P  = number of nonempty targets;
  e  = m-P = number of empty targets;
  H  = number of heavy targets with d_i>=2;
  A  = sum over heavy targets of (d_i-1), with natural subtraction;
  L  = number of light targets with d_i>=2;
  L3 = number of light targets with d_i>=3.

The exact posted left side is

  F=P+A+L+L3.

The OR-only sound private count selects A distinct heavy OR gates, all false
at Y. Select the L distinct actual nontrivial light output gates, all true
at Y; two empty-singleton-support prefix AND gates true at Y; and the NOT.
These sets are pairwise disjoint, so

  B0=A+L+3 <=N.

Every nontrivial heavy output is a distinct forced refund, so R>=H. If997
were false, integrality would give

  N+R+m <=F-1,
  delta :=N-B0 <=L3-R-e-4 <=L3-H-e-4.                 (1)

In particular L3<=H+e+3 is already impossible by the basic heavy bound.
The interesting next two layers have delta<=0 or delta<=1.


2. PRIVATE COST OF A WHOLE LIGHT OUTPUT BEFORE NOT

Suppose a nontrivial light target S_i is computed by its actual output wire
before NOT. That prefix is monotone and computes the exact linear function
OR(S_i). The sound OR-only count with empty base gives at least d_i-1 prefix
OR gates globally below OR(S_i), with at least two singleton positives each.

They are disjoint from every selected heavy OR: before NOT the replacement
and original circuits agree, so a shared gate would have at least two
singleton positives in S_i intersect S_j. They are disjoint from the two
empty-support ANDs and NOT. They are disjoint from every other nontrivial
light output, whose full target support cannot be contained in S_i under
linearity. At most the output for S_i itself is already in the B0 budget.

Thus a prefix light output requires at least d_i-2 extra gates. These extra
private sets are disjoint across different prefix light outputs. Therefore

  sum over prefix nontrivial light outputs of (d_i-2) <=delta.       (2)

This statement explicitly permits sharing inside one target's OR network;
the distinct-target disjointness follows from singleton support and linearity.


3. GENERAL SUFFICIENT CRITERIA

Criterion A. Suppose every nontrivial light target has size at least4 and

  L3<=H+e+5.

Then997 holds.

Proof. Under a failure, (1) gives delta<=1. A prefix nontrivial light output
would cost at least2 extra gates by (2), so none exists. The entire prefix
then consists of the selected heavy OR gates false at Y, the two selected
empty-support AND gates true at Y, and at most one extra gate Q. The global
residual-prefix argument in pg4-exclusion-paper.txt, Obligation4, applies
verbatim. It includes false-at-Y alternative branches and proves that the
NOT input h has a mandatory raw variable u.

Apply accepted986 to the subfamily of all targets of size at least2, with
the singleton clause T={u}. Empty and singleton targets contribute zero to
the sum of predecessors, so its conclusion is

  N >=1+sum_i(d_i-1)
    =1+A+sum over light targets of (d_i-1).

For every light target, d_i-1 is at least min(3,d_i)-1. Consequently

  N+m >=1+A+L+L3+m >=F+1.

This contradicts failure even without using a refund. This proves A.

Criterion B. Suppose every nontrivial light target has size at least3 and

  L3<=H+e+4.

Then997 holds.

The same proof works with delta<=0. Even a triple output would need one
extra gate before NOT, so all nontrivial light outputs are again excluded.

Singleton light targets may be present in both criteria: their exact outputs
can be raw variables, and any separate alias gate already consumes one of
the extra slots. Empty targets only improve inequality (1). Heavy target
sizes are unrestricted.

Criterion C (stronger paper extension, independently reviewed).
Suppose every nontrivial light target has size at least3 and

  L3<=H+e+5.

Then997 holds. This extends both criteria above by allowing a triple output
to occur before NOT when delta=1. Here is the additional argument.

Assume failure. If no nontrivial light output occurs before NOT, Criterion A's
residual-prefix argument applies. Otherwise (2) implies delta=1 and exactly
one such output can occur. Its target S has size3. Its two private OR gates
must be the output itself and the sole extra gate Q: only the output can
overlap the selected light-output budget, and there is no other spare gate.
In particular both gates are OR, Q precedes the output, Q is globally below
OR(S), and Q has at least two singleton positives.

Before Q, each selected heavy OR is a pure OR of variables outside Y. Such
an OR cannot read either true-at-Y empty-support AND. There is no other
prefix light output, no other extra gate, and no earlier NOT. The two selected
ANDs that occur before Q, if any, are conjunctions of raw variables of Y and
have empty singleton support.

Since Q is a sound OR for S, each effective operand is globally below OR(S).
A raw input contributes at most one singleton positive. A heavy OR operand
is globally below both OR(S) and its heavy target, so has at most one
singleton positive by linearity; being a pure OR, a nonempty such operand
is exactly a singleton alias. An empty-support AND contributes none. Q has
at most two operands and at least two singleton positives, so both operands
are singleton aliases of two distinct variables in S. Thus Q is exactly
the OR of a two-point subset S' of S.

No selected heavy OR after Q can depend on Q: the first such OR would contain
the two singleton positives of Q, contradicting its heavy target's
intersection-at-most-one with S. Heavy ORs also cannot depend on the true
light output or the true empty-support ANDs. Hence every selected heavy OR
throughout the prefix remains a pure outside-Y OR.

The triple output is itself an OR with singleton support exactly S. Its
potential operands are raw inputs; heavy ORs sound for S, hence singleton
aliases; empty-support ANDs; or Q. Two operands can cover all three points
only by using Q and the third singleton. Therefore the actual full output
function is Q OR x_v for v in S minus S'. Both prefix light OR atoms are
independent of the ANDs and have nested supports S' subset S.

Every true-at-Y AND in the residual prefix takes only raw variables or these
two OR atoms, possibly through the other AND. All other prefix wires are
false at Y and cannot be its operands. The complete NOT-input function h
therefore flattens to an AND of raw factors and a subset of the two nested
OR atoms. If there is no raw factor, this function is constant one, OR(S'),
or OR(S); its minimum positive weight is zero or one. This contradicts the
minimum weight3 of h. A raw factor is therefore mandatory. Applying986 as
in Criterion A finishes the contradiction.

The nested-atom argument does not apply to pair outputs. Several distinct
pair outputs can be present without consuming extra private gates, as the
example in Section5 shows.


4. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE ORDER-FOUR PROJECTIVE PLANE

For21 five-point targets and a three-point Y meeting13 targets and missing8,
Criterion A applies with L3=13, H=8, e=0. This recovers the paper exclusion
without using any projective-plane geometry after the incidence counts.

For a noncollinear three-point Y in PG(2,4), the number of light lines is12
and heavy lines9. The basic bound already suffices: L3=12=H+3. The exact
left side is12*3+9*5=81; the selected-gate bound is N>=9*4+12+3=51 and
forced refunds give R>=9, hence N+R+21>=81.

Combining the collinear and noncollinear cases with the previously derived
k<=2 argument and the d_i<=k+1 criterion for k>=4 covers every choice of Y
for the PG(2,4) set family on paper. This is a special case of997; it does
not establish the universal997 statement or a separate full line-OR lower
bound without the NOT-window hypotheses.


5. WHY PAIR TARGETS REQUIRE ANOTHER ARGUMENT

A light pair output costs no additional private gate beyond its own output,
so it can remain in the prefix even when delta=0. For example, let

  Y={y1,y2,y3},
  h=(y1 OR a1) AND (y2 OR a2) AND (y3 OR a3),

using three pair-OR outputs and two AND gates. The pairs can be disjoint,
so they satisfy the original pairwise-intersection-at-most-one assumption.
Then h has minimum positive weight3 and is true at Y, yet has no mandatory
raw variable. This is an obstruction to this residual-prefix lemma, not a
counterexample to997: the remaining outputs and refund inequality still
need to be met.

A small mandatory clause, such as {y1,a1}, still exists in this example.
Accepted986 then charges the incidence count of one surviving clause point.
Controlling that incidence cost relative to the gate surplus is a possible
next step. It must be proved globally; the pair example prevents assuming
that low surplus alone always produces a mandatory singleton.
