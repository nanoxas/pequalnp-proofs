INDEPENDENT EXCLUSION OF THE THIRTEEN-INPUT CANDIDATE

Status: paper proof, independently reviewed from the exact support equations.
The family arithmetic and support-transition superset have a reproducible
finite audit in independent_audit.py / independent-audit.json. No SAT solver
or Lean compiler was run. No SAT-UNSAT or kernel-certified exclusion is claimed.

The case has n=13, m=10, Y={0,1,2}, one heavy eight-point target
S0={3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10}, and nine light triples listed in case.json. All45 pairs
of targets meet in at most one point, and every target is a proper nontrivial
subset of the13 inputs.

Its exact counts are k=3, F=35, H=1, and

  B=sum_i(min(3,|S_i|)-1)=20.

The checked light/minterm bound gives N>=B+k=23, while forced refunds give
R>=1. A violation would require N+R+10<35. Therefore its only numerical
possibility is N=23 and R=1.

Replace the unique NOT by constant true, obtaining the monotone circuit D.
It has the same23 gates and agrees with the original circuit on all inputs
of weight below3, including every singleton. For a wire w let U(w) be its
singleton support in D. Let z be the replaced NOT wire, with U(z)=all13.
Define the same light-assigned sets as in the checked LightCount helper:

  G_i={gate wires w != z : U(w) subset S_i and |U(w)|>=2}.

Each G_i has at least2 gates. The G_i are mutually disjoint by linearity.
The checked minterm argument also supplies at least2 prefix AND gates with
empty singleton support; they are disjoint from all G_i and z. These counts
already exhaust23 gates. Therefore every G_i has exactly2 members, exactly2
other gates have empty support, and the only remaining gate is z.

Thus every gate's singleton support is one of:

  - empty;
  - all13 (only z);
  - a subset of some S_i of cardinality at least2.

Raw inputs have singleton support. An unavailable or future reference is
the constant-false effective operand, with empty support. At an OR gate,
the support is the union of its effective operand supports. At an AND,
it is their intersection, with nullary intersection equal to all13.

Consider the two gates in G0 in increasing order.

FIRST G0 GATE.
An OR yielding a subset of S0 cannot read z or a foreign G_j gate: z has
support outside S0, and a foreign support has at least2 points but intersects
S0 in at most1. With no earlier G0 gate, it must combine raw singleton or
empty operands. To have at least2 support points it is an OR of two distinct
raw variables, so its support has size exactly2.

An AND cannot be the first G0 gate. A raw operand limits its output support
to one point; an empty operand makes it empty; any foreign G_j operand limits
an output contained in S0 to at most one point. Only all13 operands remain,
which yield all13, not a subset of the proper target S0. This covers nullary
and unary gates as well as binary gates.

SECOND G0 GATE.
An OR can read only raw singleton/empty supports and the first G0 support.
It has at most two ports, so its support size is at most3. An AND cannot
use a foreign wide or raw operand and still have a support of size at least2
inside S0. Any remaining effective combination uses the first G0 support
and possibly all13, so its output has size at most2. Aliases and repeated
fan-ins cannot increase either bound.

The actual output for S0 has singleton support exactly S0, of size8, because
D agrees with the exact original output on all singleton inputs. It must be
a valid gate wire; it is neither z nor an empty-support gate. It therefore
belongs to G0. Both G0 gates have support at most3, a contradiction.

The sole possible violation size23 is thus excluded on paper. The candidate
does escape the earlier numerical criteria (n>3k+1, heavy excess4>k3, and
Q_light9>H+k+2=6), but it is settled by equality in a different checked count.
No target synthesis run is warranted for this case.

GENERALIZATION SUGGESTED BY THE SAME ARGUMENT

For a linear family of proper targets, equality N=B+k leaves only the
light-assigned classes, empty-support minterm gates, and the universe-support
replacement wire. Within a class G_i, an OR uses raw inputs or earlier
same-class supports. An AND yielding a wide support in S_i must have an
earlier same-class operand; choose it as a support-covering continuation.
The resulting ancestor DAG has singleton leaves and branches only at
same-class OR gates, so |S_i|<=|G_i|+1. Equality also gives
|G_i|=min(k,|S_i|)-1. Therefore equality is impossible if any |S_i|>k.
This general strict-slack statement is a paper extension; the specific
two-gate case above does not rely on its formalization.
