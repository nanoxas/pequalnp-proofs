2. THE COUNT AND ITS ARITHMETIC

With |Y|=3, let P count nonempty targets, e=m-P, A=sum of (d_i-1) over heavy
targets, H count nontrivial heavy targets, L count nontrivial light targets,
and L3 count light targets of size at least3. Natural subtraction is used.

The target sum is exactly F=P+A+L+L3. The selected gate classes are A private
heavy ORs, L distinct light output gates, two true-at-Y empty-singleton-support
prefix ANDs, and the NOT. These classes are disjoint by operation, support,
or value at Y, giving B0=A+L+3 <= N. Each nontrivial heavy output satisfies
both exact refund clauses, and the outputs are distinct, giving R>=H.

Under a strict failure, N+R+m <= F-1. Substitution yields

  delta=N-B0 <= L3-R-e-4 <= L3-H-e-4.

This includes empty and singleton targets correctly. Empty sets contribute
zero to F; singleton sets contribute one, paid by the m term. Distinctness
is needed only for targets of size at least2, where it follows from the
pairwise-intersection-at-most-one hypothesis.

3. RESIDUAL PREFIX WITH NO NONTRIVIAL LIGHT OUTPUT

When delta<=1 and no nontrivial light output precedes NOT, the prefix contains
only selected heavy ORs false at Y, two selected ANDs true at Y with empty
singleton support, and at most one extra gate Q. This is a global inventory
of actual prefix gates, not a statement about one chosen accepting trace.

If Q is absent, irrelevant, after NOT, false at Y, or an AND true at Y, every
gate in the true-at-Y ancestor cone of the NOT input h is an AND. A true AND
cannot read any false-at-Y wire. Flattening this entire cone gives a genuine
global conjunction of raw Y variables and possible constant-one factors.
The window excludes constant true and forces a mandatory raw variable.

If Q is an OR true at Y, every selected heavy OR is a pure OR of raw variables
outside Y. Such an OR cannot read a true Q or either selected AND, directly
or through an OR path. Before Q, the selected ANDs are raw conjunctions.
After Q, they are conjunctions of Q and raw factors, possibly with sharing
and repetition. Therefore h has the form Q^epsilon AND conjunction(T).
Nonempty T immediately gives a mandatory raw variable. If T is empty, the
only possible nonconstant case is h=Q.

In the latter case every nonzero operand of Q must itself have minimum
positive weight at least3. Raw inputs and nonzero heavy ORs have a singleton
witness. The earlier of the two selected ANDs combines at most two raw inputs
and has a witness of weight at most2. Only the later selected AND can compute
a degree-three conjunction, using both ANDs. Consequently every nonzero
operand of Q must be this same later conjunction. Repeated operands and
false-default operands do not change it. Q therefore has a mandatory variable.

This argument explicitly accommodates nullary gates, aliases, equal fan-ins,
sharing, and unavailable references. A nullary AND cannot be one of the two
empty-support ANDs when n>=3. A true nullary extra AND contributes only a
constant-one factor; a nullary OR is false. No false-at-Y OR branch is silently
discarded from a disjunction that can influence h on other inputs.

4. CRITERION C: ONE PREFIX TRIPLE AT SURPLUS ONE

A prefix output of size d requires d-1 private sound ORs of singleton support
at least2. These ORs are disjoint from the heavy selected gates, empty-support
ANDs, NOT, and other nontrivial light outputs. At most the output itself is
already selected. Across targets the private sets are disjoint by linearity.
Thus sum over prefix light outputs of (d-2) <= delta.

Assume all nontrivial light targets have size at least3 and delta<=1. If a
prefix light output exists, it is the only one, its set S has size3, delta=1,
and the two private ORs are the output itself and the sole extra gate Q.
The private count uses the output's ancestor network, so Q precedes it.

Before Q, every heavy OR is a pure outside-Y OR: earlier prefix gates are raw
inputs, other heavy ORs, or the true empty-support ANDs, which a false-at-Y OR
cannot read. Because Q is globally sound for S, each of its operands is sound
for S. A heavy operand has singleton support inside the intersection of S
with its own heavy target and therefore at most one point; being a pure OR,
it is a singleton alias or zero. A raw operand contributes one point; an
empty-support AND contributes none. Q has at least two singleton positives
and at most two operands. It follows that both operands are distinct
singleton aliases, and Q is exactly the OR of a two-point subset S' of S.

No later heavy OR can read Q, even if Q is false at Y: the first such OR would
contain both singleton positives of Q, incompatible with its heavy target's
intersection-at-most-one with S. Heavy ORs cannot read the true light output
or the true selected ANDs either. They remain pure outside-Y ORs.

The triple output is an OR. To cover its three singleton positives with two
ports, it must read Q and the third singleton. Thus the only two prefix light
OR atoms are OR(S') and OR(S), with S' subset S. They are independent of the
ANDs. The true AND cone of h consequently flattens to raw factors and these
two nested atoms. With no raw factor it is constant one or one of those ORs,
and has minimum positive weight zero or one. This contradicts h's minimum
positive weight3. A mandatory raw factor exists.

Accepted L-meta-986 with that singleton clause applies to the subfamily of
targets of size at least2. It gives N>=1+sum_i(d_i-1). This is already stronger
than the required weighted count after adding m, even with zero refunds.
It closes the contradiction in all three saturation criteria. The same
reasoning validates the PG(2,4) collinear slice with N<=49.

5. THE UNRESOLVED GENERAL STRUCTURAL STEP

The saturation argument does not settle arbitrary L-meta-997 instances.
It requires a small surplus and, for its mandatory-variable step, excludes
light pairs. Pair outputs need no private OR beyond their own output gate.
For example, with Y={0,1,2}, the three disjoint pair targets

  {0,3}, {1,4}, {2,5}

can appear as free selected output atoms in the prefix. The function

  h=(x0 OR x3) AND (x1 OR x4) AND (x2 OR x5)

uses these three pair ORs and two ANDs. It has minimum positive weight3,
is true at Y, and has no mandatory singleton variable. Its three pair
supports are linear, and all fan-ins are binary. Adding NOT(h) gives a valid
window, but this circuit is not a counterexample to997: its target sum is6,
while six gates plus three outputs' m contribution already give9.

The example blocks a blanket assertion that every saturated prefix has a
mandatory variable. A small mandatory clause still exists, but using986 with
that clause incurs incidence charges for the remaining clause points. Those
charges must be controlled by an additional global count.

For larger surplus, light/private and heavy/private gate charges can overlap
after the NOT. The light count uses the constant-one replacement and small
input windows; the heavy count uses the constant-zero replacement and
Y-plus-point inputs. A useful next theorem must either limit this overlap or
assign it to the exact refund gates. Merely knowing that one gate has two
different partial support patterns does not establish either full target
signature required by the refund predicate. This is the outstanding bridge;
the separate checked light, minterm, and heavy counts do not supply it.
