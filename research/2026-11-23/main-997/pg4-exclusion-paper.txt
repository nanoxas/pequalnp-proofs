PAPER EXCLUSION FOR L-META-997 ON PG(2,4), THREE COLLINEAR POINTS

Status: a self-contained paper argument, audited independently by the parent
and this worker. It is not a Lean-checked proof, a SAT exhaustion certificate,
or a submission to the project. The universal L-meta-997 theorem remains open.
The interrupted finite SAT query is UNKNOWN and supplies no lower bound.

The argument actually uses the following abstract set-system hypotheses.
There are21 target sets S_i, each of size5, meeting pairwise in at most one
point. A three-point set Y meets exactly13 targets and is disjoint from the
other8. No projective-plane intersection property is used beyond these facts.
For PG(2,4), each point is on5 lines; three collinear points therefore meet
their common line and4 other lines through each point, for13 in total.

We use the exact circuit conventions of the posted row: AND, OR, or NOT;
at most two input ports; unavailable references have value false; NOT reads
its first port and negates false if that port is absent. Every output is an
exact set-OR on all inputs. All NOT gates, if any, occur at the one index t.

Let N be the gate count, z the wire at n+t, and assume the row's window
hypotheses: z is true on all inputs of weight less than3 and false on Y.
Let R be the exact refund count in the posted statement. Its left side is

  F = 13*3 + 8*5 =79.

We prove F<=N+R+21 for this family. The proof has five obligations below.


PRELIMINARY: THE NOT INPUT AND ITS TWO REPLACEMENTS

The wire z is a valid NOT gate. An out-of-range wire is constantly false and
would contradict its value on the empty input. If gate t were not a NOT, then
the uniqueness condition would make the entire circuit monotone, contradicting
z(empty)=true and z(Y)=false. Its first operand is a valid earlier wire h:
an absent or unavailable first operand would make z constantly true.

The prefix computing h is monotone, and

  h(Y)=true; h(X)=false whenever |X|<3.

Thus h has minimum positive weight exactly3 and empty singleton support.
Its input wire cannot be raw or unavailable. Since h is monotone, z is false
on every input containing Y.

Let C0 replace the NOT gate by the zero-input OR (constant false), and let C1
replace it by the zero-input AND (constant true). Gate indices and counts are
preserved. C0 is monotone, is pointwise below the original circuit on each
wire, and agrees with it on all inputs containing Y. C1 agrees on all inputs
of weight less than3. These are ordinary same-index replacement inductions;
the accepted L-meta-986 source has the corresponding replacement machinery.

The singleton support U(w) of a prefix wire is the set of points p for which
that wire is true on input {p}. Before NOT, the original circuit and C0/C1
agree globally, so this definition is unambiguous.


OBLIGATION 1: OR-ONLY SOUND PRIVATE COUNT

General claim. Let D be a monotone fan-in-at-most-two circuit, T a target set,
and B a base set disjoint from T. Suppose an output wire is globally below
OR(T) and accepts B union {p} for every p in T. Then D contains at least
|T|-1 OR gates w such that:

  (a) w is globally below OR(T);
  (b) w accepts B union {p} for at least two different p in T.

Proof. Call a wire sound if it is globally below OR(T), and let V(w) be its
support on the base-plus-point inputs B union {p}. A sound wire has V(w)
contained in T: because B misses T, acceptance at B union {p} forces p in T.

At a sound OR, every operand is sound. At a sound AND, at least one effective
operand is sound. Otherwise choose, for each operand, an input avoiding T
on which that operand is true. Their union still avoids T, and monotonicity
makes the AND true there, contradicting soundness. The chosen sound operand
has base support containing the AND's base support. A nullary true AND cannot
be sound, since it accepts the empty input; unary gates and invalid false
operands obey the same argument.

Starting at the output, stop whenever |V(w)|<=1. At an AND choose the sound
child just described; at an OR retain its effective children. The resulting
finite acyclic network has unary AND continuations and binary OR branches.
The union of the stop supports covers T: support can only enlarge at a chosen
AND child and is the union of the child supports at an OR. Each stop covers
at most one point. Hence there are at least |T| distinct stops. A rooted DAG
with b binary branch nodes and any number of unary nodes has at most b+1
distinct stops, by counting one necessary incoming port for each non-root
node against two ports per branch and one per unary node. Thus b>=|T|-1.
Every expanded OR has |V(w)|>=2 and satisfies (a), proving the claim.

For different targets T,T' disjoint from the same base B and with
|T intersection T'|<=1, the charged OR sets are disjoint. A shared sound
wire's base support would be contained in T intersection T', while having
at least two points.

Apply the claim to C0 and the8 targets disjoint from Y, with base B=Y.
Each target has size5, so choose four private charged ORs per target. This
gives exactly32 distinct gates, denoted H. Every H gate is false at Y,
because it is globally below a target disjoint from Y.

The base-empty special case is also needed later: a monotone prefix computing
an exact OR of five points contains at least four globally sound OR gates
whose singleton support has at least two points.


OBLIGATION 2: THE48-GATE PARTITION BY VALUES AT Y

Let L be the13 actual output gates for the targets meeting Y. These are valid
gate wires: a raw input or an unavailable wire cannot compute an OR of five
distinct variables. They are distinct, because equal output functions would
give equal target sets, contrary to pairwise intersection at most one. Each
is true at Y, so L is disjoint from H.

There are at least two prefix AND gates with empty singleton support that
are true at Y. Here is the full minterm argument. Prune the true cone of h
at Y, stopping at any wire with nonempty singleton support, choosing one true
child at an OR, and retaining all true children at an AND with empty singleton
support. Every retained reference is valid and earlier: an unavailable
reference cannot be true. A zero-input OR cannot be true, while a zero-input
AND would have nonempty singleton support because n>=3.

If A is the number of retained empty-support AND nodes, the same unary/binary
DAG port count gives at most A+1 stops. Choose a singleton input activating
each stop. The union of these singletons activates every retained node and
h by monotonicity and gate propagation. Its weight is at most A+1. Since h
has no positive input of weight less than3, A>=2. Choose two such gates and
call their set A0. Both are true at Y and are therefore outside H. Their empty
singleton support separates them from all L outputs.

The NOT gate z is outside H (its value in C0 is constantly false), outside L
(it is true on the empty input), and outside A0 (different operation and later
than the prefix). Thus

  |H|+|L|+|A0|+1 =32+13+2+1=48 <=N.

Each of the8 heavy targets contributes a distinct forced refund. Its actual
output has singleton signature exactly that target, and on Y union {p}, for
p outside Y, has the same target signature because the target misses Y.
It satisfies both exact refund clauses. Therefore R>=8.

If the posted inequality failed, N+R+21<79, hence N+R<=57 and N<=49. Together
with N>=48, only N48 or N49 remains. In this situation H,L,A0,z account for48
distinct gates and there is at most one other gate Q in the entire circuit.
The possible numerical failures are N48 with R<=9 or N49 with R<=8.


OBLIGATION 3: NO LIGHT OUTPUT CAN OCCUR BEFORE NOT

Suppose one L output for target S_i occurred before the NOT. Its prefix is
monotone and computes exactly OR(S_i). Obligation1 with empty base gives four
prefix OR gates globally below OR(S_i), each having at least two singleton
positives. Call this private set P_i.

P_i is disjoint from H. At a prefix wire, C0 and the original circuit agree
on all inputs. A shared P_i/H wire would therefore be globally below both
OR(S_i) and a heavy target OR(S_j). Its singleton support would lie in
S_i intersection S_j and have at least two points, impossible.

P_i is disjoint from A0 by operation/support and from z by prefix order. It
is disjoint from every other light output: another full five-point target
support cannot be contained in S_i when their intersection has size at most
one. At most its own output gate can belong to both P_i and L.

Thus at least three of the four P_i gates are outside the48 already selected
gates. This contradicts N<=49. Consequently all13 light outputs occur after
NOT. This exclusion uses only linearity of the set system, not any property
that light targets intersect one another.


OBLIGATION 4: THE GLOBAL RESIDUAL PREFIX FORCES A MANDATORY INPUT

Before NOT, every gate is now one of:

  - a selected H OR, false at Y;
  - one of the two selected A0 ANDs, true at Y and with empty singleton support;
  - the one possible extra gate Q.

No NOT occurs in this prefix. Invalid references act as constant false.
The following analysis concerns the complete functions of the wires, not
merely one accepting trace. False-at-Y alternative branches are retained.

Case 1: Q is absent, lies after NOT, is irrelevant to h, or is false at Y.

The gates true at Y in h's prefix cone can only be the two A0 ANDs and raw
variables of Y. An AND true at Y cannot read a false-at-Y wire. The H gates
are false at Y and cannot turn a false branch into a true one. If Q is also
false at Y, its whole false-at-Y OR network cannot reach h through the
remaining true AND nodes. Therefore h is globally a conjunction of raw
variables. It is neither false nor constant true, by its window hypotheses,
so at least one raw variable is mandatory.

Case 2: Q is an AND true at Y.

H gates false at Y cannot read Q or A0 through OR ancestry. All gates true at
Y in the residual h-cone are ANDs. They flatten globally to a conjunction of
raw variables. Again at least one variable is mandatory.

Case 3: Q is an OR true at Y.

In this case every H gate is globally an OR of raw variables outside Y (or
constant false). It cannot read Q or an A0 gate, which are true at Y, and
there is no other prefix gate type. In particular false-at-Y alternatives
entering Q are genuine linear OR functions; they have not been dropped.

Every A0 gate before Q is a conjunction of raw variables of Y. Every A0 gate
after Q is a conjunction of Q and raw variables, possibly with repeated
factors. Consequently h has the global form

  Q^epsilon AND (AND over a raw-variable set T),  epsilon in {0,1}.

If T is nonempty, a variable in T is mandatory. If T is empty and epsilon=0,
h is constant true, impossible. The only remaining possibility without an
already mandatory variable is h=Q.

Since h=Q has minimum positive weight3, every nonzero operand of Q must also
have minimum positive weight at least3. Raw variables and nonempty linear
ORs have minimum positive weight1, so cannot be such operands. Among the two
A0 AND gates, the earlier one combines only raw variables and has a positive
witness of weight at most2. Only the later A0 gate can have a degree-three
raw conjunction, and obtaining degree3 uses both A0 gates. Thus every nonzero
operand of Q must be this same later raw-conjunction wire. Empty/invalid
operands contribute zero, and repeated operands change nothing. Hence Q
equals that raw conjunction and has a mandatory variable after all.

This completes all cases. There is a point u such that h(x)=true implies
x_u=true on every input. Since h(Y)=true, u belongs to Y.

The counts also cover unary/nullary gates. A true nullary AND in A0 would
contradict empty singleton support; a true nullary OR is impossible. Unary
AND/OR gates are aliases. If Q is a true nullary AND, it is constant one and
does not remove any mandatory factor. If Q is a unary OR, Case3 is its alias
special case. No well-formedness beyond the project's false-default semantics
is being silently assumed.


OBLIGATION 5: APPLY THE ACCEPTED SINGLETON-CLAUSE LEMMA

Use accepted L-meta-986 with T={u} and t0=u, where u is the mandatory input of
h. Its key premise is exactly that h is false whenever every variable of T
is false. All other premises hold: one actual NOT gate with a valid earlier
first operand; fan-in at most two; exact set-OR outputs; pairwise intersection
at most one; and every target has size5. The maximum-incidence choice of t0
is automatic for a singleton T. The sum over T.erase t0 is empty.

Its conclusion is therefore

  1 + sum_i(|S_i|-1) <=N,
  1 +21*4 =85 <=N.

This contradicts N<=49. Hence the supposed997 violation cannot exist. In
fact, under this Y-window hypothesis the paper argument excludes every
circuit of size at most49 for this set system; N>=50 together with R>=8 gives

  N+R+21 >=50+8+21 =79 =F.

The number85 is used only after deriving a mandatory input in the saturated
small-size case. It is not asserted as an unconditional lower bound for all
circuits satisfying the original plane problem.
