INDEPENDENT AUDIT: STRENGTHENED CLAUSE COUNT AND CROSSING PAIRS

Status: independent mathematical audit. The strengthened count and its
crossing-pair application have since passed Lean in ClauseMinterm,
OutsideIncidence, and PairClause, with standard-only axiom audits. Their
source hashes and exact scope are recorded in the corresponding verification
JSON files. This reviewer ran no additional compiler or target SAT query.

Conclusion: the strengthening and its crossing-pair application are
sound under the stated one-NOT window hypotheses. The restriction sets used
in986 do not compromise the extra gate count. Empty and singleton targets
must be excluded from the incidence degree used in the pair application,
or removed by an equivalent filtered-degree arithmetic lemma.

1. SETUP AND EXACT CLAIM

Let the target family be linear: distinct target sets intersect in at most
one point. The original circuit has fan-in at most two and AND/OR/NOT gates,
with every NOT at the distinguished index t. Let k=|Y|>=2, and suppose the
distinguished wire is true on all assignments of weight below k and false
on Y. The checked NotWindow machinery derives an actual NOT with a valid
earlier first operand h. Its monotone prefix satisfies

  h(Y)=true, and h(X)=false for |X|<k.

For a finite input set T, assume h is false whenever every input in T is
false. Equivalently h implies OR(T); this is the mandatory-clause premise.
Choose t0 in T. No hypothesis that T is itself a target is required.

The proposed strengthened inequality is

  k + sum_i(|S_i|-1) <= N + sum_{u in T\{t0}} degree(u),

where natural subtraction is used and N is the original gate count. It is
obtained by modifying986's gate-union count, not by merely adding k-1 to
the conclusion of the accepted public theorem.

2. WHY THE EXTRA PREFIX GATES ARE DISJOINT FROM986'S OWNED SETS

The986 proof uses the constant-true replacement D of the unique NOT. For
each target define

  P_i = inputs\(T\{t0})  if t0 belongs to S_i,
        inputs\T         otherwise.

Its owned gate set G_i excludes the replaced NOT wire z and requires

  support_D(w) intersect P_i subset S_i,
  |support_D(w) intersect P_i| >= 2.

The restriction enters by intersecting the GLOBAL singleton support with
P_i. It is not an independently modified circuit that could create new
singleton positives. Hence a gate with empty global singleton support is
excluded from every G_i, for every choice of P_i.

Apply the checked minterm bound in D to h. Prefix preservation gives h's
original positive value and minimum positive weight in D. Let A be the
set of empty-singleton-support AND gates true at Y at or before h. Then

  k <= |A|+1.

Every member of A precedes z. Therefore A is disjoint from z and all G_i.
The G_i are mutually disjoint by986's original argument: the P_i have only
two possible nested values, and a shared gate would provide at least two
points in an intersection of two distinct target sets.

The same original per-target count gives

  |S_i\(T\{t0})|-1 <= |G_i|.

Counting the union of A, all G_i, and {z} yields

  k + sum_i(|S_i\(T\{t0})|-1) <= N.

The unchanged finite-set deletion inequality from986 then gives the claimed
strengthening. No assumption that the prefix AND gates are live in a chosen
target output is needed: they are distinct gates in the counted circuit.
Invalid references, nullary gates, aliases, and sharing are already handled
by the checked minterm and prefix-preservation lemmas.

The final maximal-degree premise of accepted986 is unused by its proof body.
A rebuilt strengthened lemma may therefore accept ANY t0 in T. If the exact
public interface is retained, one can instead choose a maximum-degree member
of T as originally proposed; the crossing-pair bound below works either way.
One must not invoke the accepted interface with an arbitrary t0 while omitting
its stated premise merely because the implementation did not use it.

3. CROSSING-PAIR INCIDENCE BOUND

Restrict to the index subfamily I={i:|S_i|>=2}. Linearity and exact output
correctness persist. Empty and singleton targets will be restored afterward.
Write degree_I(u)=#{i in I:u in S_i} and H=#{i in I:S_i disjoint Y}.

Suppose T={a,b}, where a belongs to Y and b does not, and T is a mandatory
clause of h. The points a,b are distinct. With t0=a the clause cost is
degree_I(b). With a maximal-degree t0 it is the minimum of degree_I(a) and
degree_I(b), which is no larger than degree_I(b).

Partition the targets in I containing b into heavy and light targets.
There are at most H heavy targets. For each light target containing b,
its intersection with Y is nonempty. These intersections are pairwise
disjoint: if two shared p in Y, the original target intersection would
contain both p and b, which are distinct. Choosing one Y point from each
such target is therefore injective. Thus

  degree_I,light(b) <= |Y| = k,
  degree_I(b) <= H+k.

This argument does not assume that {a,b} is a target, nor that two light
targets intersect elsewhere. It uses b outside Y essentially.

Combining it with the strengthened clause count gives

  k + sum_{i in I}(|S_i|-1) <= N+H+k,
  sum_{i in I}(|S_i|-1) <= N+H <= N+R,

where R is the exact posted refund count. The last inequality is the checked
forced-refund bound: every nontrivial heavy output supplies a distinct refund.

For the full family, terms |S_i|-1 are zero outside I. If P counts nonempty
targets, then sum_i |S_i| = P + sum_i(|S_i|-1), and P<=m. Consequently

  sum_i |S_i| <= N+R+m.

Each weighted term in997 is at most |S_i|. Thus the complete posted997
inequality follows in the crossing-pair clause case. This conclusion is
stronger than the required weighted inequality for that case.

Filtering is significant: repeated singleton targets are allowed by the
intersection hypothesis and can make degree(b) arbitrarily large. They
contribute zero to the predecessor sum and should not be charged in the
clause-deletion cost used for this application. Filtering to I avoids this
pitfall exactly. Empty targets contribute nothing and only increase m.

4. CONJUNCTIONS OF PAIR-OR ATOMS

Assume, as a GLOBAL functional identity, that h is a conjunction of raw
variables and OR atoms of size at most two. This assumption cannot be
replaced by agreement on Y or by one accepting trace.

A raw factor is a mandatory singleton clause and already gives the desired
bound through986 (or the strengthened version with zero clause cost).
Repeated endpoints in a pair are raw factors. Constant-one factors may be
deleted; a false factor is impossible because h(Y)=true.

Suppose there is no raw factor. Every remaining clause consists of two
distinct inputs, and every clause meets Y because h(Y)=true. If no clause
is a crossing pair, every clause has both endpoints in Y. Removing any one
point from the nonempty set Y leaves every two-point clause satisfied.
Then h is true on an input of weight k-1, contradicting the global minimum
positive weight k. Therefore a crossing-pair clause exists.

Thus a conjunction of pair-OR atoms is fully covered, including the former
three-disjoint-pair obstruction to a mandatory singleton. The proof does
not require the pair atoms to be members of the target family.

5. LIMITS AND NEXT STRUCTURAL OBLIGATION

This does not show that an arbitrary pre-NOT monotone function h has a
mandatory crossing pair. For example,

  h=(x0 AND x1 AND x2) OR (x3 AND x4 AND x5) OR (x6 AND x7 AND x8)

has minimum positive weight three. Every mandatory clause must hit each of
the three disjoint monomials and therefore has at least three variables.
Such a prefix has no mandatory singleton or crossing pair.
This is a limitation of the reduction, not a counterexample to997.

The strengthened clause inequality offers a more general sufficient condition:
if an arbitrary mandatory clause T and choice t0 in T have deletion cost at
most R+k, the full unweighted target-sum bound follows. A weighted version
can additionally absorb the light truncation slack and the empty-target
allowance. The remaining task is to produce a clause with a controlled
incidence cost, or to count the gates needed by prefixes whose mandatory
clauses are all expensive. The new bound resolves pair atoms but does not
yet supply that general structural step.
