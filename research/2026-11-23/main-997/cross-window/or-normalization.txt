COMMON OR-ONLY NORMALIZATION FOR A LINEAR DISJUNCTION FAMILY

Status: paper proof, independently reviewed by inspect_project. This file
does not contain a new Lean verification or solve unrestricted L-meta-997.
The checked OrPrivateCount already gives the corresponding OR-count lower
bound; the point here is an actual common deletion history.

1. STATEMENT

Let a monotone AND/OR circuit compute outputs OR(S_i), where any two
distinct target sets intersect in at most one input. Constants and repeated
or missing ports are permitted, with missing reads interpreted as false.
There is an OR-only circuit for the same output family whose OR gates are
a subset of the original OR gate identities. Rewiring can change their
operand lists. No original gate is duplicated or new binary gate added.

The construction may use false as a free boundary constant. It can be
represented by an unavailable wire in the project semantics; alternatively
keep a pre-existing false wire while counting it separately. A final exact
OR output is false at zero, so a constant-true wire cannot survive in its
OR-only cone.

2. ELIMINATE THE LAST LIVE AND

Prune gates outside all output cones. Choose the live AND with the largest
index. Every later gate on a path from it to an output is now OR, so the
chosen AND has an OR-only downstream cone.

Write A for its singleton-positive support.

If A is empty, replace its value by false. If A={p}, replace it by input p.
Monotonicity makes this a pointwise decrease. The replacement agrees with
the gate on every singleton assignment, hence every output retains exactly
its former singleton positives. Outputs cannot become larger. A monotone
function bounded above by OR(S_i) and true at every singleton in S_i is
exactly OR(S_i), so all outputs remain correct.

If |A|>=2, every OR-only reachable output contains A in its target. Linearity
therefore permits only one such target S_i. On the complement of S_i, that
output is false, so the chosen AND is false. At least one operand is false
there. Choose that operand f. Since f is monotone, f<=OR(S_i) globally.
Bypass the AND through f. This raises the old AND value, preserves every
previous positive output, and keeps the unique affected output bounded by
OR(S_i). Other outputs are unaffected. All outputs remain exact.

A nullary AND is true and cannot have an OR-only path to an exact OR output,
which is false at zero. An unavailable read is false; a gate with a wide
singleton support cannot have such a false operand. Unary ANDs are aliases.

After the bypass, remove the AND, redirect its uses to the chosen earlier
wire, and prune newly dead gates. Repeating terminates because the live AND
count decreases. Remaining repeated-input ORs, one-input aliases and false
inputs can also be simplified without adding a gate.

3. APPLICATION TO THE Y=1 RESTRICTION

In997, set every variable in Y to true. The monotone NOT predecessor h is
then true and z=NOT h is false. Every light output is constant true and is
discarded; every heavy output remains the exact disjunction of its target,
whose variables all lie outside Y. The remaining circuit is monotone on
those outside variables, so Section2 applies to the heavy linear family.

Consequently all AND gates, the NOT, constant contexts, aliases and dead
gates can be excluded from a common retained heavy OR circuit. A heavy OR
forest still requires sum_heavy(|S_i|-1) branching gates. The quantitative
goal left for997 is to show that the removed identities supply at least

  sum_light(min(|Y|,|S_i|)-1) - R

credits, with natural subtraction and the exact original refund R. The
private OR/AND dichotomy pays AND-rich light classes directly through
removed AND identities. OR-full light classes require the remaining work.

4. OWNERSHIP MUST BE TRACKED THROUGH REWIRING

Retained identities need not retain their original functions or singleton
supports after earlier gates are bypassed. In particular, original global
C0 soundness and original refund equality need separate justification.

There is a useful one-sided invariant: on each singleton of the remaining
outside variables, the value at every surviving identity never decreases.
A wide-AND bypass is a pointwise increase. A support0/1 replacement agrees
with its gate on every singleton, so all later singleton values remain
unchanged in that step. Dead pruning and alias renumbering preserve values
at surviving identities. Hence the original shifted support V(w) is a
subset of the final OR support at a retained gate w. This does not assert
that the original full gate function is bounded by that final disjunction.

If a retained OR has a final wide support inside heavy T and its original
C1 support U lies inside light S, the invariant gives

  U(w) intersect V(w) subset S intersect T,

so its stable original support has at most one point. This connects the
deletion history to the original paired signatures without assuming that
rewiring leaves them unchanged.

The original overlap ORs also known to be C0-sound for T are false both at
zero and at Y. Their OR-only predecessors have those same two false base
values, until a raw input or an AND entry is reached. The unfinished task
is an injective or amortized count of those entries and the context gates
removed by the normalization, including the other retained identities for
which only shifted-support containment has been proved.

Heavy-only OR gates can also be true at zero and false at Y. Those gates
have universal C1 singleton support and do not lie in a proper light private
class. They are safe retained capacity, so the assertion that every heavy
retained OR is false at both base inputs would be too strong.
