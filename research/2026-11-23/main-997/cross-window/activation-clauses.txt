1. Exact semantic information beyond the small-weight window
-----------------------------------------------------------
Work in the k>=2 branch; SmallWindow already settles the smaller values.
Write h for the monotone predecessor of the unique NOT, z=not h. Let C1 and
C0 replace z by true and false, respectively. The same-index replacement
lemmas give equality with the original circuit at every wire on h=0 and
h=1, respectively. All three circuits have the same gate indices and count.

For every target S and every input X avoiding S,

  h(X)=0 implies C1_output_S(X)=0.                 (1)

This holds at arbitrary input weights. The familiar negative window is the
consequence of (1) and the minimum positive weight k of h. Moreover C0's
output is globally bounded above by OR(S), because C0 is pointwise below
the original circuit. For a heavy target, C0 agrees with OR(S) at every
input containing Y, and Y avoids S.

The actual output function of C1 satisfies

  OR(S) <= C1_output_S <= OR(S) OR h.

The left inequality follows from singleton correctness and monotonicity;
the right inequality is (1). This identity of domains is stronger than
agreement only below weight k. It does not assert equality of internal
wire functions under replacement outside their respective domains.

2. A bounded frontier that forces h
----------------------------------
Use the current private class G_i, consisting of valid non-z gates whose
C1 singleton support has size at least two and is contained in S_i. Let W
be the exceptional wide gates outside every private class. Linearity makes
the private classes disjoint. Define the boundary valuation

  base(w)=1 exactly when w=z or w belongs to W,

and evaluate only the gates of G_i using cutValue.

If this cut output is false, the checked false-frontier argument in
PrivateDichotomy supplies the full private OR quota |S_i|-1. Thus when
the private OR count is strictly smaller, the cut output is true.

Apply the checked positive admissible frontier to that true cut output.
It gives a frontier F disjoint from G_i, all of whose base values are true,
with

  |F| <= privateAND_i+1.

Hence F is contained in {z} union W. Put B_i=F minus {z}. For every actual
input X avoiding S_i, if all C1 wires in B_i are true at X, then z is also
true in C1 and the admissible truth-transfer theorem makes its target
output true. By (1), h(X) must be true. Therefore

  X disjoint S_i and every b in B_i true in C1 at X
      imply h(X)=1,                               (2)
  B_i subset W,  |B_i|<=privateAND_i+1.

The input X need not have weight below k. The same fixed frontier B_i
works simultaneously for every X in (2). This fixed-witness quantifier is
important for any later charging argument.

Every b in B_i has a singleton positive outside S_i: otherwise its support
would place it inside G_i. Choosing one outside singleton per b constructs
an activating input. Thus (2) supplies a nonempty h-positive input avoiding
S_i. In particular, B_i cannot be empty, because h(0)=0.

3. Consequence for every clause of h
-----------------------------------
Let T be any input set with h(complement T)=0; equivalently, the monotone
clause OR(T) is implied by h. Put X=complement(T union S_i). Monotonicity
gives h(X)=0, and X avoids S_i. By (2), some b in B_i is false at X.

Write U(b) for its C1 singleton support. A point p in U(b) outside both T
and S_i would make b true at singleton p and hence at X, a contradiction.
Consequently

  for every clause T of h, there exists b in B_i with
       empty != U(b) minus S_i subset T.           (3)

Nonemptiness uses b in W. Equation (3) is a necessary refinement condition
against every clause of h. It is stronger than merely requiring |B_i|>=k.
It does not say that each U(b) minus S_i is itself a clause of h: the
existential b may depend on T. Reversing that implication would be unsound.

4. Target-specific avoidance threshold
--------------------------------------
If h has a positive input avoiding S_i, let lambda_i be the minimum weight
of such an input. Choosing one outside singleton witness per b in B_i and
using (2) gives

  lambda_i <= |B_i| <= privateAND_i+1.

Thus every target satisfies the stronger semantic alternative

  privateOR_i >= |S_i|-1,
  or privateAND_i >= lambda_i-1.                 (4)

If there is no h-positive input avoiding S_i, the first alternative is
forced. Equivalently S_i is a clause of h, and (1) makes the C1 output
globally equal to OR(S_i).

For a heavy target, Y is a positive h-input avoiding it, and the global
minimum weight is k. Hence lambda_i=k exactly. For light targets lambda_i
can be k, larger than k, or absent. The full negative-input information
therefore strengthens the light-side AND credit while preserving the
known k-1 heavy-side credit.

If z itself belongs to the chosen full frontier F, removal gives the
slightly stronger |B_i|<=privateAND_i. This is a conditional observation
about that frontier; no claim is made that z always belongs to it.

5. Relevance and remaining obstruction
--------------------------------------
The checked C0 heavy OR count and C1 private credits give two independent
resource families. The finite quota audits show that overlap cannot in
general be paid by the exact refund count or by refunds plus direct NOT
readers alone. Even selecting only residual quotas can require unused
gates outside those two resource families.

Equations (2)-(3) retain the common h-domain information that a support-only
owner graph discards. A possible global proof would charge the necessary
context contributions or private-frontier requirements while respecting
shared prefix gates. Neither a per-frontier activation threshold nor (3)
alone supplies such a disjoint accounting. In particular, the same h and
the same exceptional wire may participate in many target frontiers.

The finite switched-circuit examples use h=AND(Y). They already satisfy a
stronger checked mandatory-singleton-clause bound. They refute proposed
selection shortcuts, and leave the full inequality safe with ample unused
gate capacity. The checked OneHeavyDeficit theorem also settles all examples
with only one deficient heavy class, including the residual and all-operation
pool obstructions. A useful unrestricted invariant must measure shared
capacity or context contributions when at least two heavy classes remain
deficient.
