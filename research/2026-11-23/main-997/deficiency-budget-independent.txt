1. DIRECTED SEPARATION

Fix a deficient private class G_i and the full cut used to produce its
missed-point set M_i. For p in M_i, let D_p consist of the cut's outsider
terminals true at singleton p. The cut property says that every boundary
wire true at singleton p is either the constant-true z or a member of D_p.

Let X avoid S_i and have s<k points. Let A(X) be all outsiders true at X.
Suppose |D_p minus A(X)|<k-s. Every remaining outsider w has a singleton
positive outside S_i: otherwise its full singleton support would lie in
S_i, placing it in G_i instead of W. Choose one such outside point per
remaining outsider, and adjoin these points to X.

The resulting input V avoids S_i and has cardinality at most

  s+|D_p minus A(X)|<k.

Every terminal in D_p is true on V, by monotonicity: it was already true
on X or is activated by its chosen singleton witness. z remains true.
Therefore all cut terminals that were true at singleton p are true on V.
Cut transfer makes the output true on V, contradicting its negative-window
specification. It follows that

  |D_p minus A(X)|>=k-s.

For X={q} with q outside S_i, write A_p={w in W:p in support(w)}.
Since D_p is a subset of A_p, the result gives

  |A_p minus A_q|>=k-1.

Repeated chosen singleton witnesses only lower the input weight. No
distinctness, global normalization, proper-target condition, or gate-shape
assumption is needed in this argument. Constant-true outsiders are already
active and cause no exception.

DeficientSeparation meets the formalization requirement by exporting the
same witness M_i with this singleton separation conclusion. The earlier
statement that each p in M_i merely activates at least k outsiders does
not logically imply the directed-difference inequality by itself. The
general X formulation above remains explanatory; the checked assembly
needs only the singleton case.

2. THREE DEFICIENT CLASSES FORCE r>=3k-3

Assume three distinct deficient classes i,j,l. For each one choose a point
of its M-set outside the other two targets. Each other target removes at
most one point, since M_i is contained in S_i and the target family is
linear. The bound |M_i|>=k+1 supplies a surviving point for k>=2, and in
particular for the paper's k>=3 range.

These three points are pairwise distinct and each lies outside the other
two targets. Directed separation therefore applies in both directions to
each pair of their active-outsider sets A_1,A_2,A_3. Thus

  |A_i symmetric_difference A_j|>=2(k-1)

for all three pairs. Each outsider contributes either0 or2 to the sum of
the three symmetric-difference cardinalities. Consequently

  6(k-1)<=sum_{i<j}|A_i symmetric_difference A_j|<=2r,

so r>=3k-3. Therefore r<=3k-4 permits at most two deficient classes.

The threshold is sharp for this set-distance argument. Partition W into
three disjoint blocks B12,B13,B23, each of size k-1, and define

  A1=B12 union B13,
  A2=B12 union B23,
  A3=B13 union B23.

Then r=3k-3, every directed difference has cardinality k-1, and every
|A_i|=2k-2>=k for k>=2. Thus even the popular-point lower bounds coexist
with equality at this boundary. This is a sharpness example for the
abstract set inequalities; it is not asserted to come from a circuit.
Excluding three classes at r=3k-3 needs additional circuit information.

3. CHECKED DEFICIT ARITHMETIC

Let D=sum_i max(|S_i|-1,0), G=union_i G_i, and let delta_i be the positive
private deficit of class i. Internal disjointness of the G_i gives

  D<=|G|+sum_deficient_i delta_i.

The checked OrCompensation and PrivateAnd bounds give, for each deficient
class,

  delta_i<=|W_OR|-|G_i_AND|<=|W_OR|-k+1.

Handle the case of no deficient classes first: D<=|G| already suffices.
Otherwise delta_i>=1 forces |W_OR|>=k, so the displayed integer expression
is nonnegative. With at most two deficient classes,

  total deficit<=2(|W_OR|-k+1)
               <=2r-2k+2
               <=r+k-2
               <=r+k,

where the third inequality uses r<=3k-4. The checked private/minterm budget
|G|+r+k<=N then gives D<=N. The weighted target sum F is at most D+m,
including empty and singleton targets, so F<=N+m<=N+R+m.

This calculation has two units of slack at r=3k-4. It does not by itself
increase the allowed r, because the separate no-three-class argument stops
at that boundary.

4. FAILURE UNDER Eplus<=4k-3

Write B=sum_i max(min(k,|S_i|)-1,0), H for the number of nontrivial heavy
targets, and R for the exact refund count. The existing weighted comparison
and forced-refund theorem give

  F<=B+H+Eplus+m<=B+R+Eplus+m.

A strict failure F>N+R+m therefore implies N<=B+Eplus-1. The private
classes satisfy |G|>=B, while |G|+r+k<=N. Combining these inequalities gives

  r<=Eplus-k-1.

Under Eplus<=4k-3, this is r<=3k-4, precisely the range in Section2.
This yields the sufficient criterion proved in QuadrupleExcess. The
assembly uses the exact refund predicate and permits empty targets.

5. REVIEW RESULT AND COMPLETED FORMALIZATION

No mathematical gap was found in directed separation, the three-class
distance argument, or the final arithmetic. The arithmetic
qualification is the explicit no-deficient-class branch above, avoiding a
negative expression 2(|W_OR|-k+1) when no deficit exists.
