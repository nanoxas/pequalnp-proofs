PARTIAL CHARGE OVERLAP DOES NOT IMPLY A WHOLE-SET REFUND

Status: EXHAUSTIVE_DATA_AUDIT_PASS for the explicit seven-input circuits.
All128 truth rows, the exact guarded refund predicate, and every circuit
and per-set hypothesis in the proposed L-meta-1596 row are checked by
audit.py. No SAT solver or Lean compiler was used for these examples.
This refutes particular overlap-count shortcuts. It does not refute1596
or997: every example satisfies their stated conclusion.

1. THE CIRCUIT

Use inputs y0=0,y1=1,y2=2,a=3,b=4,c=5,d=6. Put Y={0,1,2},
light target S={0,3,4}, and heavy target T={3,5,6}. Their intersection is{3}.
There are two targets, both of size3, and k=|Y|=3.

The base circuit, with zero-based gate indices, is:

  gate0 = y0 AND y1
  gate1 = gate0 AND y2                 (h)
  gate2 = NOT gate1                    (z)
  gate3 = b AND z
  gate4 = c AND y0
  gate5 = a OR gate3
  gate6 = gate5 OR gate4               (g)
  gate7 = g OR y0                      (light output)
  gate8 = a OR c
  gate9 = gate8 OR d                   (heavy output)

Thus g=a OR(b AND z)OR(c AND y0). If y0 is true, the light output is
true. If y0 is false, h is false and z is true, so the light output is a OR b.
Consequently the light output is exactly OR(S) on every assignment. The
heavy output is exactly OR(T) by construction.

The NOT is true on all29 assignments of weight less than3 and false at Y.
Every gate has at most two inputs and gate2 is the sole NOT. All gates are
structurally live in the union of the two output cones.

2. EXACT SUPPORTS AND REFUNDS

Write U(w) for the original circuit's singleton-positive support, and
V(w) for the set of points p outside Y for which w is true at Y union{p}.
The exact proposed per-set classes are

  L_S={gate w : U(w) subset S and |U(w)|>=2},
  H_T={gate w : V(w) subset T and |V(w)|>=2}.

The base circuit has

  L_S={5,6,7}, H_T={6,8,9}, L_S intersect H_T={6}.

For g=gate6, U(g)={a,b} and V(g)={a,c}. Both are proper two-point subsets
of their respective three-point targets. The gate is not a refund: the
posted refund requires its entire singleton support to equal some target,
and its entire shifted outside support to equal a heavy target. Neither
condition is supplied by membership in these partial classes.

The only refunded gate is gate9, the exact heavy output. Gate7 has singleton
support S, but its shifted outside support is all{a,b,c,d}, which is not T.
Gate8 has both supports{a,c}, which is not a whole target. The AND prefix
gates and the NOT do not supply additional refunds.

This remains an obstruction even if one additionally asks the common gate
to be false at Y: g(Y)=false. It also has the expected normalized soundness:
after z is replaced by true it is globally below OR(S); after z is replaced
by false it is globally below OR(T). Thus those soundness conditions do not
turn a partial overlap into the exact whole-set refund.

3. ARBITRARILY MANY LIVE PARTIAL OVERLAPS

Insert r aliases after gate6, each of the form OR(previous,previous), and
let the light output read the last alias. The heavy OR chain remains
independent. Every alias is structurally live and has the same two supports
as g. All hypotheses still hold. The exact counts are

  circuit gates N=10+r,
  |L_S|=|H_T|=3+r,
  |L_S intersect H_T|=1+r,
  |L_S union H_T|=5+r,
  refund count R=1.

Already r=1 disproves |L_S intersect H_T|<=R. With r=6, the total number
of eligible memberships is18, while N+R=17. Thus the global shortcut
sum_i|all eligible gates for i|<=N+R is false as well. The exhaustive audit
checks r=0,1,6,10; the formulas describe every natural r.

The weighted left side in the exact theorem stays6. Its right side is
N+R+m=13+r, so the actual theorem remains satisfied with increasing slack.
Dead-cone pruning alone does not remove this example: all aliases are live.
They are functionally redundant, which illustrates why counting every
eligible gate loses control over surplus.

4. THE MINIMUM QUOTAS CAN BE SELECTED DISJOINTLY HERE

Each target only needs two selected gates: its weighted demand3 minus1.
For every r the explicit selections

  A_S={5,7+r}, B_T={8+r,9+r}

are subsets of L_S and H_T, respectively, have cardinality2, and are
disjoint. They avoid every problematic overlap. The selected circuit charges
total4, which is all that is needed before adding the two per-target units.

5. PRECISE GAP A SELECTION LEMMA MUST FILL

Let c_i=max(min(k,|S_i|)-1,0) for light targets and
c_i=max(|S_i|-1,0) for heavy targets. Linearity makes the eligible classes
pairwise disjoint within the light family and within the heavy family:
two positives cannot lie in an intersection of size at most1. It does not
make classes from different families disjoint.

One sufficient, presently unproved selection statement is: choose exactly
c_i eligible gates for each target so that every doubly selected gate is
an exact refund. This would give total selected charges<=N+R. The example
shows that the statement must concern chosen quota witnesses, not every
gate satisfying the partial-support predicate. It does not establish that
this particular strong selection theorem always holds.

A weaker sufficient target permits unused gates to pay for unrefunded
overlap. Let A be the union of selected light witnesses, B the union of
selected heavy witnesses, W=A union B, and O=A intersect B. Because each
family is internally disjoint, the total selected charge is exactly

  |W|+|O|.

The needed global inequality is therefore

  |O| <= R + (N-|W|).

For example, an injection of the unrefunded double charges into unused
gates, with refunded overlaps paid by their existing refunds, would suffice.
More generally one can formulate a capacitated charge matching with one
ordinary credit per gate and one additional credit per exact refund.

The posted per-set count inequalities by themselves assert only that each
eligible pool is large enough. They provide no such simultaneous selection
or matching. Any proof of1596 that sums those pool sizes must obtain this
missing global constraint from the circuit's causal structure or its
low-weight negative rows. Whole-support equality cannot be inferred from
the two subset predicates.
