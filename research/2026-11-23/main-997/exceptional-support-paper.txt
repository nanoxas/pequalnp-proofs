1. SETUP

Assume the original997 hypotheses and k=|Y|>=2. Let C1 replace the unique NOT
wire z by constant true. It is monotone, has the same gate count N, and every
output computes OR(S_i) on every input of weight less than k. Let U(w) denote
its singleton support. The output supports are exactly S_i.

The elementary support-parent argument in Section2 assumes every target is
proper. This holds automatically in any remaining
case with an oversized heavy target: that target is disjoint from nonempty Y
and has at least3 points; a universal target would intersect it in more than1.
The preferred cut proof in Section3a avoids properness entirely.

Put c_i=min(k,|S_i|)-1, B=sum_i c_i, D=sum_i(|S_i|-1), all with natural
subtraction. Define the disjoint private classes

  Gi={gate w!=z : |U(w)|>=2 and U(w) subset S_i}.

An exceptional wide gate is a gate w!=z of support size at least2 that belongs
to none of the Gi. Denote their number by r. A selected set A of k-1 prefix
ANDs has empty singleton support, so is disjoint from every Gi, the r
exceptional gates, and z. Therefore

  N >= sum_i|Gi| + r + k >= B+r+k.                 (1)


2. THE ONE-OUTSIDER SUPPORT LEMMA

If r<=1, every wide AND in Gi has an earlier Gi child. All effective input
supports must contain the AND's at least two output positives. A private
child from Gj therefore forces j=i by linearity. A raw or empty-support
child is impossible. If no child is private, the only wide possibilities
are z and the unique outsider b. Their intersection is either universal
or U(b). Universality is incompatible with proper S_i; U(b) subset S_i
would classify b into Gi. This is a contradiction.

At an OR in Gi, every wide child is automatically Gi, since its support is
contained in the parent's proper S_i support. Selecting a same-class child
at each AND and both children at each OR yields a unary/binary support-cover
DAG whose singleton stops cover S_i. It needs at least |S_i|-1 OR nodes.
Hence |Gi|>=|S_i|-1, and summing with A and z gives

  N>=D+k.                                         (2)

This settles997 outright because its left side is at most D+P, where P is
the number of nonempty targets and P<=m.


3. SUPPLEMENTARY NORMALIZATION ARGUMENT: r<k SUFFICES

The pure set claim in Section2 can fail with two outsiders: their intersection
may become a private wide support. The actual low-weight negative rows give
an additional constraint that is absent from that set abstraction.

First normalize every gate with singleton support of size zero or one.
Replace an empty-support gate by constant false; replace a singleton-support
gate with support{p} by raw input x_p. Such replacements decrease its complete
monotone function and preserve its values on every singleton input. Perform
the replacements in topological order. The singleton values of every later
wire are therefore unchanged, so all support classes and outsider supports
are unchanged on retained gates.

Every output remains correct on inputs of weight<k: an originally negative
input stays negative because all functions decrease, and a positive input
contains some target singleton whose output remains true. Monotonicity then
preserves that positive input. The replacement is only a proof construction;
no gate-count increase is needed, since constants and aliases can be wired
away. z is constant true in C1 and is treated as the constant1 source.

Fix a proper target S_i of size at least2. After normalization, each Gi gate
can use only these types of effective inputs:

  - earlier Gi gates;
  - raw singleton inputs in S_i or constant0;
  - z=1;
  - exceptional wide gates.

Indeed a foreign private Gj input is impossible at an AND because all output
positives would lie in S_i intersect S_j, and impossible at an OR because
the foreign wide child support would be contained in S_i. An OR cannot read
an outsider or z, since its own proper S_i support would contain that child's
wide support. An AND cannot read a narrow input while retaining width>=2.

For each exceptional wide gate b choose p_b outside S_i with p_b in U(b).
Such a point exists: otherwise U(b) subset S_i would put b in Gi. Let

  X_i={p_b : b exceptional}.

Then X_i avoids S_i and has size at most r. In the normalized monotone circuit,
every exceptional b is true on X_i, because it remains true on singleton p_b.
If r<k, low-weight correctness forces the i-th output to be false on X_i.

Now keep only the Gi gates and their dependencies within Gi; replace every
exceptional input, and z, by constant1. Keep raw S_i inputs and constant0.
This is a monotone subcircuit with at most |Gi| gates. On raw S_i=0 it exactly
reproduces the normalized circuit's Gi values on X_i, because all exceptional
inputs there are1. Thus its output is false at zero. On every singleton
p in S_i, forcing exceptional inputs to1 can only increase the original
normalized output, which was already true. The subcircuit is therefore
exactly OR(S_i) on every assignment: monotonicity gives every nonzero input,
and the zero input is false.

A binary-gate circuit computing OR of d distinct essential variables needs
at least d-1 gates, even allowing constants, repeated ports, unary gates,
and sharing. For example, its live source/gate DAG has d distinct input
sources and one output; counting ports gives d<=number_of_gates+1.
Consequently |Gi|>=|S_i|-1. Empty and singleton targets contribute zero and
need no argument. Summing proves (2) whenever r<k.

The construction is performed separately for each target. It does not claim
that one simultaneous circuit can force every outsider globally to1 while
preserving all outputs. Separate per-class witnesses are enough for the
cardinality bounds, because the Gi are disjoint.


3a. PREFERRED DIRECT-CUT PROOF, WITHOUT NORMALIZATION OR PROPERNESS

Fix target S_i. If its size is at most1 the desired bound is trivial. Assume
for contradiction |Gi|<|S_i|-1. Apply the existing cut lemma from LightCount
to Gi at output o_i. It gives a frontier F disjoint from Gi, of size at most
|Gi|+1, with the usual truth-transfer property.

For each frontier wire w set its clean label to empty if w=z or w is an
exceptional wide gate; otherwise set it to U(w) intersect S_i. Every clean
label has size at most1. To prove this, suppose the intersection had at least
two points. The support_gate helper makes w a valid gate wire. Since it is
not z or exceptional, some target S_j contains U(w). If j=i, w belongs to Gi,
contradicting frontier disjointness. Otherwise the two clean positives belong
to S_i intersect S_j, contradicting linearity. Raw inputs and invalid wires
are automatically covered by support_gate's contrapositive.

The union of clean labels has size at most |F|<|S_i|, so choose p in S_i
outside that union. For every exceptional gate b, choose one singleton
positive p_b outside S_i; this is possible directly from its definition.
Let X be their union. Its size is at most r<k, it avoids S_i, and monotonicity
makes every exceptional gate true on X. z is true there by hypothesis.

Every frontier wire true on singleton p is true on X. The z and exceptional
cases transfer as just explained; in every other case, truth on singleton p
would put p in the missed clean label, a contradiction. The cut transfers
the true output on singleton p to X. Low-weight correctness says the output
on X is false because X avoids S_i, contradiction.

This establishes |Gi|>=|S_i|-1. It needs no rewriting, AND/OR parent case
split, or properness assumption. In particular if S_i is universal, the
exceptional set must be empty and X is empty; the same proof applies.
Other constant-true gates are handled automatically by their support class
or exceptional membership. No special zero-true case is missing.


4. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE EXCESS CRITERION

Let H be the number of nontrivial heavy targets, e=m-P, and

  E=sum_heavy max(|S_i|-k,0),
  Eplus=sum_heavy max(|S_i|-k-1,0).

The exact forced refunds give R>=H and E<=H+Eplus. A violating circuit would
satisfy

  N <= B+P+E-R-m-1 <= B+Eplus-e-1.                (3)

If Eplus<=2k+e, combine(1) and(3) to get r<=k-1. Section3a then gives
N>=D+k, contradicting violation. The simpler condition Eplus<=2k therefore
suffices without mentioning empty targets.

If there is no oversized heavy target, E=0 and the already checked light
bound settles997 directly. Otherwise properness holds as explained above.
The k<=1 branches are already handled separately by SmallWindow.

OutsideActivation.lean now checks the r<k argument, and DoubleExcess.lean
checks the original997 conclusion under Eplus<=2k. Thus a remaining
counterexample needs at least k exceptional wide gates and Eplus>2k. The
slightly stronger empty-target adjustment Eplus>2k+e is retained here as an
independently reviewed paper consequence; it is not the statement of the
checked DoubleExcess theorem.


5. A TRANSVERSAL CONDITION STRONGER THAN r<k

For a fixed target S_i, Section3a only needs an S_i-avoiding input X of weight
less than k on which every exceptional wire is true. A sufficient condition
is that the family of sets

  {U(b) minus S_i : b exceptional}

has a hitting set of size less than k. Thus the number r can be replaced,
per target, by the transversal number of these outside singleton supports.
One singleton can activate many exceptional gates, and support inclusions
can make some outsiders irrelevant to this hitting-set count. In particular
only inclusion-minimal outside supports need to be hit.

If this transversal condition holds for every target, all full private
bounds |Gi|>=|S_i|-1 hold and the full count(2) follows. If it holds only for
some targets, the full bound can be used on those classes and the original
min(k,|S_i|)-1 bound on the rest. The classes remain disjoint, so these
individual improvements sum directly.

This formulation captures the useful dependency/forest idea semantically:
an OR outsider with an outsider child is activated whenever that child's
singleton witness is activated, while intersecting outsiders may create a
new inclusion-minimal support. A pure set count of such intersections does
not itself prove an additional gate charge. The low-weight negative witness
is the step that rules out a cheap private entry.


6. HISTORICAL r=k ROUTE: OUTSIDER CLASSIFICATION AND PROJECTED SUPPORT

Status: PAPER_REVIEWED, retained as research history. The specific outsider
classification and projected-support DAG extraction below were not
formalized. They are no longer needed: the direct-cut proof in Section7 is
checked and covers the larger range r<=2k-2, including r=k for k>=2.

Assume r=k>=2. If every |Gi|>=|S_i|-1, the full count is already proved.
Otherwise choose a deficient class i and apply the cut in Section3a. Choose
p in S_i missed by its clean-label union. Let D be the exceptional frontier
wires true on singleton p. If |D|<k, activating only D by outside-singleton
witnesses gives the same contradiction as Section3a. Hence D is the entire
exceptional set E: all k outsiders lie on this frontier and all accept p.

Write A_b=U(b) minus S_i. These nonempty sets are pairwise disjoint. A shared
outside point for two distinct outsiders, together with one witness for each
of the other k-2 outsiders, would activate the entire exceptional frontier
on an S_i-avoiding input of weight at most k-1, contradiction.

Every choice of one point from each A_b is consequently a size-k set X that
avoids S_i and activates every outsider. The cut makes C1's output_i true
on X, whereas the original circuit's exact OR output is false. If the
original NOT wire were true on X, replacing it by true would preserve every
wire, contradiction. Therefore the NOT input h is true on every such X.
The global minimum positive weight k of h makes each transversal a minterm.
Every missed p belongs to all outsider supports, so the common inside support
contains the whole set of points missed by the cut's clean labels.

Now exploit disjointness of the outside supports. Every outsider is an OR:

  - A narrow AND operand would make the outsider's support narrow.
  - A private operand would put the outsider support inside a target,
    contradicting exceptional membership.
  - An outsider operand at an AND would make A_b a nonempty subset of that
    other outsider's A, contradicting pairwise disjointness.
  - If only z operands remain, or the AND is nullary, its support is universal.
    Its outside part contains every other nonempty A, again a contradiction.

An outsider OR cannot read another outsider, since it would contain that
other outsider's outside support. It cannot read z for the same reason.
The existence of any outsider also ensures all target sets are proper:
a universal target would contain every wide support and leave no outsiders.

For any j different from i, every wide AND in Gj has a Gj child. The only
remaining way to avoid a private same-class child would be two outsiders
(possibly with z). Distinct outsiders intersect only inside S_i, so their
intersection cannot have at least two points inside S_j. Repeated outsiders,
z with an outsider, or only z yield an exceptional or universal support,
not a proper Gj support. Thus the usual private support DAG gives

  |Gj| >= |S_j|-1  for every j!=i.                 (4)

For class i, build a support-cover DAG allowing both Gi gates and outsiders,
and label each wire by U(w) intersect S_i. At a Gi AND with a Gi child, follow
that child. If there is no such child, the gate uses two distinct outsiders;
follow either one, whose projected support contains the AND's support.
At a Gi OR retain its children. An outsider is an OR, so retain its children
as well. Stop at projected supports of size at most1.

Every expanded node belongs to Gi or E. A foreign private class projects to
at most one point by linearity; raw and narrow wires do as well. z is never
selected: Gi ANDs choose a same-class or outsider child, and neither Gi ORs
nor outsider ORs can read z. Acyclicity is inherited from circuit order.

The singleton-sized stop labels cover S_i. There are at most |Gi|+k expanded
nodes, with unary continuations at selected ANDs and at most two children at
ORs. The binary-source port count therefore gives

  |S_i| <= |Gi|+k+1.                              (5)

Combine(4),(5), the k outsiders, the original k-1 empty-prefix AND charge,
and z. The possible private deficit in class i is at most k, paid exactly
by the k exceptional gates outside all private classes:

  N >= sum_j|Gj| + k + k >= sum_j(|S_j|-1) + k.

This paper route settles997 whenever r<=k. In the numerical criterion, a violation
under Eplus<=2k+1+e forces r<=k by(1),(3); hence the convenient sufficient
condition strengthens from Eplus<=2k to Eplus<=2k+1. The projected-DAG
extraction remains paper-level; the weaker no-e conclusion is now included
in the checked stronger result below.


7. CHECKED DIRECT-CUT EXTENSION: r<=2k-2 AND Eplus<=3k-1

Status: LEAN_KERNEL_CHECKED in BoundaryActivation.lean, BoundaryRigidity.lean,
and TripleExcess.lean. The source, object, dependency and log hashes are
recorded in their adjacent .verification.json files. All checks exited0
without warnings and all audited theorems use only the standard three axioms.
This worker independently reviewed the statements and semantic bridges.

First, a cut through Gi together with all exceptional gates gives

  |S_i|-1 <= |Gi|+r.                              (6)

The remaining frontier has no exceptional wire. Every non-z frontier wire
has at most one singleton positive inside S_i, by the same clean-label
argument as Section3a. If the frontier were smaller than S_i, a missed
singleton would transfer to the zero input: z is already true, and no other
frontier wire accepts the missed singleton. This contradicts output_i(0)=0.
No outsider operation classification or circuit normalization is involved.

Now suppose Gi is deficient, meaning |Gi|<|S_i|-1. Use its fixed cut frontier
F with |F|<=|Gi|+1. Let Q be the union of its clean labels, and M=S_i minus Q.
For every p in M, define D_p to be the exceptional frontier wires true on
singleton p. If |D_p|<k, one outside-S_i witness per member of D_p would
activate every frontier wire true on p at an S_i-avoiding input of weight<k.
The cut would make output_i true on that negative input. Hence |D_p|>=k.

Pick one p0 in M. Its at least k outsider wires have empty clean labels, so
the clean-label union can use only F minus D_p0. Consequently

  |Q| <= |F|-|D_p0| <= |Gi|+1-k,
  |M| >= |S_i|-|Gi|-1+k >= k+1.                  (7)

Thus a deficient target contains at least k+1 points, each activating at
least k global exceptional gates.

Assume r<=2k-2. For another target S_j, linearity supplies a p in M outside
S_j: otherwise at least k+1 points would lie in S_i intersect S_j. This
singleton activates at least k outsiders. For each still inactive outsider,
choose one additional singleton positive outside S_j. Their union with p
avoids S_j, activates every outsider, and has size at most

  1+(r-k) <= k-1.

The output is negative on this input by low-weight correctness. The direct
activation cut therefore proves |Gj|>=|S_j|-1. Every class other than i is
full; (6) pays the one possible deficit by r. Summing gives the checked bound

  sum_i(|S_i|-1) <= sum_i|Gi|+r.                 (8)

Together with the original k-1 empty-prefix ANDs and z, (8) gives
N>=sum_i(|S_i|-1)+k. This implies the requested refund inequality because
its weighted left side is at most sum_i(|S_i|-1)+m.

Finally, a putative violation with Eplus<=3k-1 combines(1),(3) to force
r<=2k-2. The previous paragraph contradicts it. TripleExcess's final theorem
therefore proves the exact original997 conclusion under the sole additional
numerical condition Eplus<=3k-1 (and k>=2, with the smaller k cases already
checked separately). It uses the original guarded refund predicate and
does not change the public statement. The full unrestricted997 task remains
open. An extra +e refinement follows on paper from(3), but is not claimed as
the exact statement of the checked TripleExcess theorem.


8. CHECKED DEFICIENCY BUDGET: r<=3k-4 AND Eplus<=4k-3

Status: LEAN_KERNEL_CHECKED for k>=3. The new chain consists of
OrCompensation, DeficientSeparation, DeficientPacking, CoupledCut, PrivateAnd,
and QuadrupleExcess. All current sources passed with no warnings and only
the standard three axioms in their recorded audits. The accompanying
deficiency-budget-paper.txt and deficiency-budget-independent.txt explain
the proof and its independently reviewed arithmetic.

For a deficient class Gi, DeficientSeparation retains a missed-point set
Mi of cardinality at least k+1. For p in Mi and q outside Si, write Ap for
the exceptional gates true at singleton p. A target-avoiding activation
argument gives

  |Ap minus Aq| >= k-1.                            (9)

If three classes are deficient, choose one missed point from each outside
the other two targets. Linearity permits these choices. Applying (9) in
both directions gives three pairwise symmetric differences of size at
least 2(k-1). Each exceptional gate contributes zero or two to their sum,
so 6(k-1)<=2r. Hence r<=3k-4 permits at most two deficient classes. This is
the checked DeficientPacking conclusion.

Let W_OR be the exceptional OR gates and Gi_AND the AND gates in Gi.
OrCompensation proves

  |Si|-1 <= |Gi_OR|+|W_OR|,
  delta_i := |Si|-1-|Gi| <= |W_OR|-|Gi_AND|.        (10)

CoupledCut constructs a full frontier and its positive subfrontier together.
The positive frontier stays inside the full one and has cardinality at
most one plus the number of private AND gates. A missed singleton point
activates only z and exceptional terminals on this frontier. If fewer than
k-1 private AND gates existed, outside singleton witnesses would activate
the frontier on fewer than k points avoiding Si. This contradicts the
negative window. PrivateAnd therefore proves

  |Gi_AND| >= k-1                                  (11)

for every deficient class. The simultaneous frontier construction resolves
the boundary-inclusion obligation from the earlier paper draft.

If there is no deficient class, summing the full private counts finishes
immediately. Otherwise a positive deficit in (10)-(11) implies |W_OR|>=k.
With at most two deficient classes their total deficit is bounded by

  2(|W_OR|-k+1) <= 2r-2k+2 <= r+k-2 <= r+k.

Thus the checked private/minterm budget yields

  sum_i (|Si|-1) <= sum_i |Gi|+r+k <= N.          (12)

Natural subtraction handles empty and singleton targets. The weighted
left side of the posted statement is at most sum_i(|Si|-1)+m, so (12)
implies the exact refund inequality.

Finally, a violation under Eplus<=4k-3 would combine (1) and (3) to force
r<=Eplus-k-1<=3k-4. QuadrupleExcess checks this original-circuit assembly
with the exact guarded refund predicate. SmallWindow already covers k<=2.
The additional +e refinement remains a paper observation. The stronger
global private OR/AND dichotomy was subsequently checked and is recorded
in Section9. No unrestricted L-meta-997 proof is claimed.


9. CHECKED PRIVATE DICHOTOMY AND THE ONE-HEAVY-DEFICIT CASE

CutEvaluation formalizes arbitrary boundary evaluations of a private gate
set and frontiers for either Boolean value. Its positive frontier branches
only at AND gates, while its negative frontier branches only at OR gates.
Unavailable references, constants, repeated ports and nullary gates keep
their actual circuit semantics.

PrivateDichotomy applies this to the boundary that sets z and every
exceptional wide gate true, and all other boundary wires false. If its
output is false, the negative frontier covers all target singleton points,
yielding |Si|-1 private OR gates. If its output is true, the positive
frontier can be activated outside Si using at most privateAND_i+1 points.
The negative input window therefore forces at least k-1 private AND gates.
The checked alternative is

  |Si|-1 <= privateOR_i  OR  k-1 <= privateAND_i.

It holds for every target class under the stated singleton positives and
target-avoiding negative-window hypotheses. It strengthens PrivateAnd,
whose conclusion was conditional on a deficit in the total private count.

OneHeavyDeficit addresses the weighted demand directly. Light targets use
only min(k,|Si|)-1 private gates. Every nondeficient heavy target already
pays |Si|-1. A single deficient heavy target can use the compensated bound
|Si|-1<=|Gi|+r, and the r exceptional gates are outside all private classes.
Thus at most one deficient heavy class implies the stronger conclusion

  weighted target sum <= N+m,

without refund credits and without any upper bound on r. The original-
circuit bridge covers every window size, using SmallWindow for k<=2.
Any failure of the exact L-meta-997 inequality must therefore have at least
two deficient heavy C1 private classes. All three new modules passed on
their frozen sources with standard-only axiom audits.

The completed quota-audit examples show why further selection rules must
account for resources outside the C1-private/C0-heavy pools. They satisfy
the original inequality and do not contradict this checked theorem. The
all-h=0 activation/clause invariant in cross-window/activation-clauses.txt
is a separate paper consequence; the shared global accounting remains open.
