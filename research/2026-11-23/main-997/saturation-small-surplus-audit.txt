ARBITRARY-WINDOW SATURATION WITH ZERO OR ONE EXTRA GATE

Status: paper proof, independently reviewed by the bounds and inspection
workers. The complete residual-prefix classification below has not been
formalized in Lean. The constituent minterm, private-count, replacement,
and strengthened-clause lemmas have separate checking records. No new SAT
query or compiler run was made for this audit. Published checkpoint files
and staging directories remain unchanged.

RESULT

Assume the exact L-meta-997 circuit/output/window hypotheses and k=|Y|>=2.
Let d_i=|S_i|, and let

  H = #{i : S_i disjoint Y and d_i>=2},
  e = #{i : d_i=0},
  Q = sum over i with S_i meeting Y of max(min(k,d_i)-2,0).

Then the following is sufficient for the posted inequality:

  Q <= H+k+e+2.                                             (S)

The +1 version uses the zero-surplus case. The +2 version also uses the
one-extra-gate argument. No lower bound on nontrivial light target sizes
is imposed; pair outputs are explicitly allowed. The mandatory-pair clause
need not be one of the targets. The result is conditional on the full exact
output/window hypotheses; it is not a general assertion about arbitrary
monotone circuits or a proof of unrestricted997.


1. THE GATE PARTITION AND EXACT SURPLUS ARITHMETIC

Call a target heavy if it is disjoint from Y, light otherwise. Put

  A = sum over heavy targets of (d_i-1),
  L = #{i : S_i meets Y and d_i>=2},
  P = #{i : d_i>0}, so m=P+e.

All subtractions displayed as counts are truncated natural subtraction.
The posted left side F satisfies the exact identity

  F=P+A+L+Q.                                               (1)

For a heavy target, d=(d-1)+1 if d>0, and both terms are zero if d=0.
For a light target, min(k,d)=1+1_{d>=2}+max(min(k,d)-2,0).
Light targets are automatically nonempty.

Let N be the circuit size, z the actual NOT output, and h its valid earlier
operand. The window hypotheses give h(Y)=true and h(X)=false for |X|<k.
Choose the following disjoint sets of actual gate indices:

  H0: exactly A private heavy OR gates, false at Y in C0;
  L0: the L distinct actual outputs of nontrivial light targets, true at Y;
  B0: exactly k-1 prefix AND gates, true at Y with empty singleton support;
  {z}: the unique NOT gate.

C0 is the false replacement of the NOT. Before z, its values agree globally
with the original circuit. On Y, it agrees with the entire original circuit.
The heavy OR gates are globally sound for their assigned heavy targets in C0.
The private heavy family count supplies H0. The minterm count supplies B0.
The four classes are disjoint by value at Y, singleton support, or operation.
No least-circuit or liveness hypothesis is being used.

Their total size is

  A+L+(k-1)+1=A+L+k <= N.

Write N=A+L+k+delta. Let R be the exact posted refund count. Every nontrivial
heavy output is a distinct refund, so R>=H. If the posted inequality failed,
integrality and (1) would imply

  N+R+m <= F-1,
  delta <= Q-R-e-k-1 <= Q-H-e-k-1.                         (2)

Thus Q<=H+k+e+1 forces delta=0, while (S) forces delta<=1. It remains to show
that either surplus case gives a mandatory singleton or crossing pair for h.


2. PRIVATE COST OF A PREFIX LIGHT OUTPUT

Suppose an actual nontrivial light output lies before z and computes OR(S_i).
Its monotone prefix contains at least d_i-1 OR gates globally sound for S_i,
each with at least two singleton positives. These gates lie in its ancestor
network. They are disjoint from H0: before z, the original circuit and C0
agree, and a shared gate's two singleton positives would lie in the
intersection of S_i with a distinct heavy target. They are also disjoint
from B0, z, and every other nontrivial light output. At most the output for
S_i itself is already in L0.

These private sets are pairwise disjoint across distinct prefix light
outputs, again by singleton support and linearity. Therefore

  sum over prefix nontrivial light outputs of (d_i-2) <= delta.     (3)

When delta=0, every such output is a pair. When delta=1, they are pairs with
at most one triple. Any triple consumes the entire extra private-OR budget.
This claim does not require every pair output itself to be an OR gate: a
pair output implemented by an alias can consume the spare gate instead.

For the prefix analysis below, an actual light output may be substituted
globally by its exact raw-input OR function. This is a functional identity
on every assignment. It removes dependence on its internal implementation,
even if that implementation contains an empty-support AND or the extra gate.
It is not an approximation obtained from an accepting trace at Y.


3. CONJUNCTIONS OF PAIR ATOMS

We use the following elementary semantic fact. Suppose a function f is
globally a conjunction of raw variables and ORs of two-element sets, is true
at Y, and has no positive input of weight below k=|Y|>=2.

A raw factor gives a mandatory singleton. A pair factor containing exactly
one Y point gives a mandatory crossing pair. If neither occurs, every factor
is a pair contained entirely in Y. Removing any one point from Y preserves
every such pair OR, contradicting the minimum weight k. Thus f has a
mandatory singleton or crossing pair.

An empty conjunction is constant true and violates the minimum weight.
Repeated endpoints are raw factors. False factors cannot occur when f(Y)
is true. Constant-one factors can be dropped. These observations cover
nullary gates and aliases in the applications.

The strengthened clause bound plus the outside-incidence count proves the
full unweighted target-sum inequality whenever h has either clause type.
For a crossing pair {a,b}, choose a in Y and b outside Y. After excluding
singleton/empty incidences, degree(b)<=H+k; the k term in the strengthened
clause bound cancels this k, while H<=R. See
crossing-pair-independent-audit.txt and the checked ClauseMinterm interface.


4. ZERO SURPLUS

Before z, every gate is in H0, L0, or B0. Equation (3) allows only pair
outputs in L0 before z. The H0 gates are ORs false at Y, the B0 gates are
ANDs true at Y, and all prefix L0 outputs are globally exact pair ORs true
at Y.

After substituting the pair outputs, traverse the true-at-Y cone of h.
Every internal non-atom gate is an AND. Such an AND cannot read a false-at-Y
wire, so no H0 gate can be an operand in this cone. Consequently h is
globally a conjunction of raw/pair atoms. Section3 supplies the clause,
and the posted inequality follows. This proves the +1 criterion for every
k>=2, including arbitrary numbers of pair outputs.


5. ONE EXTRA GATE WITH A PREFIX TRIPLE

Assume delta=1 and a prefix triple output for a target S exists. It is the
only prefix output of size at least3. The two private ORs required for S
must be the output itself and the sole extra gate q. Therefore q is an OR,
precedes that output, is globally sound for S, and has at least two singleton
positives. All other prefix nontrivial light outputs are pairs.

Before q, every selected heavy OR is a pure OR of raw variables outside Y:
it is false at Y and cannot read a selected AND or light output true at Y.
There is no other extra gate. A true-at-Y selected AND before q is a
conjunction of raw/pair atoms and has empty singleton support.

Every operand of the sound OR q is itself globally sound for S. A raw input
contributes at most one singleton positive. A heavy OR operand is a pure
linear OR sound for both S and its heavy target; by linearity, its support
has at most one point. Another light pair output cannot be a nonzero wide
sound operand for S, because its two-point support intersects S in at most
one point. A selected empty-support AND contributes no singleton positives.

Since q has at most two ports but at least two singleton positives, its two
effective operands must be distinct singleton aliases. Thus q is exactly
OR(S') for a two-point subset S' of S.

No later selected heavy OR can read q, even if q is false at Y. The first
such OR would contain both singleton positives of q, contradicting its
heavy target's intersection-at-most-one with S. Heavy ORs also cannot read
the true light outputs or selected ANDs. They remain pure outside-Y ORs.

After substituting the exact light outputs and q, h is globally a conjunction
of raw factors, pair ORs (including possibly OR(S')), and at most one triple
OR(S). If a raw or crossing-pair factor occurs, the clause argument applies.
Otherwise every pair factor lies inside Y. Such factors survive deletion
of any Y point. The single triple factor can fail under deletion of at most
one Y point: only when its intersection with Y is a singleton. Because
k>=2, choose a different point to delete. Every factor stays true, giving
a forbidden positive input of weight k-1.

Thus the prefix-triple case also has the required singleton/crossing pair.
The argument works for all k>=2 and does not discard a false-at-Y q branch.


6. ONE EXTRA GATE WITHOUT A PREFIX TRIPLE

All prefix L0 outputs are pairs. Substitute them globally by pair-OR atoms.
Call the sole possible extra gate q. If q is absent from the prefix, irrelevant
after substitution, an AND, or false at Y, the true-at-Y cone of h consists
only of ANDs and raw/pair atoms. False-at-Y wires cannot enter this AND cone.
Even if a false q affects an actual pair-output implementation, that output
has already been replaced by its exact global pair function. Section3 applies.

The only remaining case is an OR gate q true at Y which actually occurs in
the contracted cone of h. Every selected heavy OR is then a pure outside-Y
OR, because it cannot read q, a selected AND, or a light output, all true at Y.
There are exactly k-1 selected AND gates available outside the contracted
light outputs, and no other extra gate.

Since q is the only non-atom OR, h has the global form

  h=q AND M,

where M is a conjunction of raw/pair atoms. This representation comes from
the contracted AND cone; any repetitions are harmless. If M has a raw or
crossing-pair factor, that factor is a mandatory clause of h. Suppose it has
neither. Then all factors of M are pairs contained in Y. Hence

  M(Y\{p})=true for every p in Y.

The minimum positive weight of h therefore forces

  q(Y\{p})=false for every p in Y.                        (4)

Choose an effective operand u of q which is true at Y. Such an operand exists
because q is a true OR, and it is a valid earlier wire. Equation (4) implies

  u(Y\{p})=false for every p in Y.                        (5)

Before q, the true-at-Y cone of u consists of selected ANDs and raw/pair atoms.
Stop at each raw/pair atom. Each stop is true at Y and can become false on
Y\{p} for at most one p: raw variables have one possible critical point;
a pair has one if it meets Y once, and none if it lies inside Y.

Every p in Y must have a stop that becomes false, by (5) and AND semantics.
There are therefore at least k distinct stop wires. A connected acyclic
network of binary-or-unary AND gates with b internal gate nodes has at most
b+1 distinct stops. Thus this cone contains at least k-1 selected AND gates.
It uses ALL of the available k-1 such gates. In particular u is the last
selected AND, and every other selected AND is a strict ancestor of u.

No selected AND remains after q. In the contracted representation, h cannot
be a raw/pair atom (minimum positive weight at most one), a heavy OR (false
at Y), or a later selected AND (none remains). Because q occurs in its cone,
we conclude h=q.

Now consider any other effective operand v of q. All false-at-Y alternatives
are retained here. If v is nonzero and is a raw input, pair output, or heavy
OR, it has a positive singleton witness. Since h=q, that witness makes h
true, contradicting k>=2. If v is a selected AND different from u, it lies
before u and its contracted AND cone has at most k-2 selected AND nodes.
It has at most k-1 raw/pair stops. Choosing one singleton witness for each
stop and taking their union yields a positive input of weight at most k-1
for v and hence for h=q, again a contradiction.

Thus every other operand is either identically zero or the same u. A repeated
operand or absent false-default port changes nothing: q=u. Finally u is a
conjunction of raw/pair atoms with minimum positive weight k. Section3 supplies
a mandatory singleton or crossing pair. This closes the extra-OR case.

The gate-count exhaustion is essential. Without it, a second disjoint
degree-k term could survive as a genuine OR alternative. We do not assert
that a small positive witness exists for an arbitrary false-at-Y branch.
Here every such alternative is explicitly a linear base atom or an earlier
selected AND with the bounded contracted cone described above.


7. CONSEQUENCE AND REMAINING LIMIT

Every assumed violation under (S) forces delta<=1 by (2), and every such
prefix has a mandatory singleton or crossing pair by Sections4--6. The
strengthened clause/forced-refund bound then contradicts the violation.
This establishes the stated paper sufficient criterion.

The result improves the complementary heavy criterion Q<=H+k+e by two.
It includes the earlier three-point PG(2,4) saturation slice and removes
the earlier restriction that all nontrivial light targets have size at least3.
It does not resolve arbitrary larger surplus, where several extra OR/AND
gates or multiple larger prefix light outputs can create genuine alternative
terms. That structural case remains open in this analysis.

Independent-review note: the inspection worker separately checked the
deletion-critical atom count, exhaustion of the k-1 selected AND gates,
the small-witness bound for every other OR operand, and the prefix-triple
private-OR argument. No flaw was found. This review is mathematical evidence;
it is not a substitute for a future Lean proof of the whole classification.
