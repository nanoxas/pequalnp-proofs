Independent review of the binary-field Walsh / few-NOT upper-bound strategy
==========================================================================

Conclusion
----------
The proposed construction is mathematically sound for the Desarguesian
projective plane PG(2,q), q=2^h, h>=1. In the usual circuit model with binary
AND/OR, unary NOT, constants and unrestricted wire fanout, it gives the
incidence Boolean function a circuit of size O(q^2 h^2) using at most2h+1
NOT gates. Equivalently, with N=q^2+q+1 points and equally many lines, the
size is O(N log^2 N) and the NOT budget is ceil(log2(N+1)).

This is a paper construction with an explicit end-to-end Boolean compiler,
including a sorting-based inverter of size O(N log^2 N). The latter was
derived by root and independently audited and implemented here. The faster
published O(N log N) inverter is an optional alternative. The local audits
validate finite instances of the complete construction. There is no Lean
certificate for this upper bound.

1. Function and geometric model
-------------------------------
Let z_p be point-selection bits and y_L line-selection bits. The target is
  F(z,y) = OR_L (y_L AND OR_{p incident L} z_p).
Only the point-side preprocessing uses arithmetic and input complements.
The line-selection variables enter the final positive AND/OR network.

Affine points are (x,y) in GF(q)^2. The q slopes a have affine lines
  L_(a,b) = {(x, a*x+b): x in GF(q)},
and the vertical family is L_(infty,b)={(b,y):y in GF(q)}. Add one infinity
point per direction, and the line containing all q+1 infinity points.
This gives N=q^2+q+1 points and lines, each line having q+1 points.
The field axioms give the projective-plane incidence properties directly.

The code supplies irreducible-polynomial representations for h=1,...,6.
The mathematical family for arbitrary h chooses any binary basis of GF(2^h).
This statement concerns the Desarguesian family, not every projective plane
of the same order or every C4-free bipartite incidence relation.

2. Exact Walsh-slice identity in an arbitrary binary basis
---------------------------------------------------------
Identify the additive group of GF(q) with F_2^h. Let <u,x> be the ordinary
binary coordinate dot product, and define
  Zhat(u,v) = sum_(x,y) (-1)^(<u,x>+<v,y>) z_(x,y).
The unnormalized length-q^2 Walsh transform in the code is exactly this
two-dimensional transform: the index x*q+y concatenates the two h-bit blocks.

For fixed a, multiplication x -> a*x is an F_2-linear map M_a. Let
tau_a(v)=M_a^T v, characterized by <tau_a(v),x>=<v,a*x>.
Then
  Zhat(tau_a(v),v)
    = sum_(x,y) (-1)^<v,a*x+y> z_(x,y).
Applying an unnormalized one-dimensional Walsh transform in v gives
  sum_v (-1)^<v,b> Zhat(tau_a(v),v)
    = sum_(x,y) z_(x,y) sum_v (-1)^<v,a*x+y+b>
    = q * sum_x z_(x,a*x+b).
The last step is character orthogonality on F_2^h. The vertical slice
Zhat(v,0) similarly yields q*sum_y z_(b,y).

Thus the inverse slices divided by q are exact affine line sums. Adding
the point at infinity for that direction gives projective line sums, and
the final infinity line is the sum of the infinity-point inputs.

The transpose in the code is necessary for a general chosen binary basis.
Replacing it by ordinary a*v without a suitable self-dual pairing would
not be justified. No field trace or special basis assumption is needed here.

3. Fixed wiring and arithmetic work
-----------------------------------
The forward transform uses 2q^2*h additions/subtractions. The q+1 inverse
transforms together use (q^2+q)*h. Hence the transform total is exactly
  T=(3q^2+q)*h.
The code's transform operation count omits the extra infinity-point
additions, infinity-line sum and final Boolean tests; these contribute only
O(q^2) word operations and O(N*h) Boolean gates.

The field products and transposes compute WIRE INDICES from fixed parameters
a,v,h. They never multiply two data-dependent point-input words. For a fixed
circuit size, all those indices are fixed constants and become wires.
Therefore no data-gate cost for field multiplication is hidden in the claim.
This is a nonuniform Boolean circuit family; a chosen field representation
also permits an effective generator, but no stronger uniformity claim is
needed for the size result.

4. Word size and division audit
-------------------------------
On Boolean inputs, each forward Walsh intermediate has absolute value at
most q^2. Each inverse-slice intermediate is a signed sum of at most q such
values, so its absolute value is at most q^3. This simple bound suffices.

With B=3h+2, signed two's-complement words represent
  [-2q^3, 2q^3-1],
which includes all those values. Modulo2^B addition and subtraction therefore
agree with the signed integer calculations throughout. After an inverse
slice, the actual integer value is q times a line sum: it is nonnegative,
at most q^2, and divisible by q=2^h. Removing the lowest h bits is consequently
exact division. There is no rounding, general division circuit, or reliance
on division in a ring where q would be invertible.

The infinity-point additions and infinity-line sum are at most q+1 and also
fit. B is conservative; minimizing it is unnecessary for the asymptotic
claim. The independent compiler uses ordinary ripple-carry addition,
two's-complement subtraction, and this fixed right shift.

5. Boolean size and NOT compression
-----------------------------------
A B-bit addition/subtraction has a binary AND/OR/NOT circuit of O(B) gates.
Nonzero tests are ORs of the output bits, so all line-hit bits can be computed
by an unrestricted Boolean network of size
  S=O(B*T+N*B)=O(q^2*h^2).

For each internal signal w, dual-rail conversion maintains w and its
complement. An AND becomes AND on positive rails and OR on negative rails;
an OR uses the reverse pair; a NOT swaps rails. At most two monotone gates
replace each original binary gate, and internal NOT gates need no gates.
This network requires only z_p and not-z_p at its inputs.

Beals--Nishino--Tanaka provide an N-input inverter of size O(N log N) using
ceil(log2(N+1)) NOT gates. Supplying all point-input complements with that
one shared inverter gives the claimed bound. The line inputs y_L remain
positive; after the point-side network, N AND gates and N-1 OR gates compute
F. Inverting all2N inputs would waste one NOT in this calculation.

For q=2^h>=2,
  q^2 < N+1=q^2+q+2 <=2q^2,
so ceil(log2(N+1))=2h+1 exactly. The inverter overhead O(N log N) is absorbed
by O(q^2*h^2).

This is an UPPER bound on the NOT budget sufficient for this implementation.
It is not a lower bound on the NOT count needed for near-linear size by
other methods. The target function itself is monotone and has a larger
zero-NOT circuit.

5a. Explicit sorting inverter: no external implementation gap
--------------------------------------------------------------
The following construction independently supplies all point complements
with the same exact NOT count, using O(n log^2 n) total gates. This is enough
for the overall Walsh upper bound, and is now part of boolean_audit.py.

Choose k=ceil(log2(n+1)) and M=2^k>n. Pad the n inputs with constant zeros
to length M, and run a bitonic sorting network using monotone comparators
  low=a AND b, high=a OR b.
Retain all original comparator input wires a,b. If the input weight is w,
the sorted outputs supply thresholds T_j=[w>=j] for1<=j<M. Set T_0=1,
T_M=0; the latter is valid because w<=n<M. Initially their complements
are known at the endpoints: Z_0=0 and Z_M=1.

For r=k-1,k-2,...,0, consider every dyadic interval [lo,hi] of length
2^(r+1), with mid=(lo+hi)/2. Complements at the endpoints were obtained
at earlier levels. Form
  bit_r = OR_intervals (T_mid AND Z_hi),
and use one NOT gate to obtain z_r=not bit_r. Exactly one interval contains
w; bit_r is true precisely when w lies in that interval's upper half,
so it is exactly binary bit r of w. Define
  Z_mid = Z_lo OR (z_r AND Z_hi).
If w<lo, this is true; if w>=hi, it is false; inside the interval it equals
z_r, which is true exactly when w<mid. Hence Z_mid=not T_mid. Induction
over the levels gives complements of all sorted wires using exactly k NOTs.

Reverse the sorting comparators in reverse execution order. If their
outputs' complements are known, the identities
  not a = not high OR (b AND not low),
  not b = not high OR (a AND not low)
recover the input complements using four monotone gates. The identities
follow directly from low=a AND b and high=a OR b. All original positive
wires remain available; all new gates are appended later, so no cycles
are introduced. Reversing all comparators yields every original complement.

For the standard bitonic network on M=2^k wires there are
  C=M*k*(k+1)/4
comparators. Sorting costs2C gates, reversal costs4C, and all threshold
levels together cost4(M-1) gates including their k NOT gates. The total is
therefore exactly6C+4(M-1), with k NOTs. Constants are free in this count;
charging constant0 and constant1 adds at most2 gates, and no NOT gates.

For n=N=q^2+q+1 and q=2^h, M=2q^2 and k=2h+1. The explicit inverter size is
  q^2*(12h^2+18h+14)-4.
Together with the unoptimized ripple-carry and dual-rail implementation,
the full compiler produces exactly
  q^2*(174h^2+168h+48) + q*(54h^2+144h+64) +60h+29
gates, plus at most2 charged constants. This formula is checked against
the generated q=2,4,8 circuits. The leading term is174q^2h^2, so the explicit
construction has the claimed asymptotic even without the published faster
inverter. No novelty claim is made for the sorting-inverter method.

6. Relation to the exact arithmetic ladder
------------------------------------------
The local checked LadderSupport theorem proves, under the recurrence's
posted base and step equations,
  G(r,0,N)=0 iff q+1<2^r.
For q=2^h, the recurrence is positive through r=h and identically zero from
r=h+1 onward. This is a structural limitation of that exact recurrence;
changing its arithmetic estimates cannot extend its support.

The Walsh/inverter construction supplies a near-linear upper bound by
r=2h+1. The interval between the recurrence's cutoff and that upper bound
is therefore a natural place to examine new invariants or better circuits.
No threshold optimality is established. In particular, any proposed bound
Omega(q^(2+epsilon)) for a fixed epsilon>0, uniformly through r=2h+1, would
contradict this upper bound on the binary-field Desarguesian family for all
sufficiently large q. Polylogarithmic superlinear lower bounds remain
compatible with O(q^2 log^2 q).

7. What was checked locally
---------------------------
Root's radon_probe.py checks exact matrix columns for q=2,4,8,16. Because
the transform and direct summation are linear, equality on all basis
vectors certifies the complete rational/integer matrix identity at those
fixed orders. It also checks all point assignments for q=2 and random
inputs for q=32,64. These finite tests do not prove the arbitrary-h result;
section2 gives the mathematical argument for that result.

The independent boolean_audit.py in this directory builds actual binary
gate networks, including B-bit arithmetic, dual-rail conversion, and the
explicit sorting inverter. It uses independently written polynomial
long-division field multiplication. The inverter was checked exhaustively
for every n=0,...,10. Its complete-circuit checks are:
  q=2: all16,384 assignments to the14 point+line variables;
  q=4: all441 singleton point-line pairs, two extreme assignments,
       and256 random full assignments (699 total);
  q=8: two extreme assignments and256 random full assignments.
Every exact line sum, every complementary rail, every line-hit output and
the final selected-incidence output matched direct evaluation. The complete
circuits have respectively2173,19701,143617 gates and3,5,7 NOTs, with constants
free; add at most2 gates if constants are charged.

The compiler intentionally uses a simple, unoptimized arithmetic network.
Its concrete small-order gate counts do not claim an improvement on the
small monotone circuits under current desk investigation. The published
O(n log n) inverter is cited rather than implemented; the implemented
O(n log^2 n) alternative suffices for the complete asymptotic construction.
No Lean file for the Walsh construction, Boolean compilation, or inverter
was checked here.
